[59/S0/F0] Majuli is the world's largest inhabited river island, located in eastern India on the river Brahmaputra and surrounded by the Brahmaputra and its tributary.
[59/S0/F1] The objective of the Majuli case study is an immersive, interactive virtual reality tour in which visitors can experience the island's ecological heritage and tangible and intangible cultural heritage, and each information point reveals related information for interactive exploration.
[59/S0/F2] The Majuli virtual tour contains a large number of geographically spread information points, including satras (Vaishnavite monasteries), villages, tribal villages, towns, and other places of interest, and each information point contains many points of interest, creating two levels of extensive content.
[59/S0/F3] The Majuli virtual tour uses a combination of navigation techniques, with walking within each information point and teleportation or a vehicle metaphor between points, because the island's large geographical spread means a single technique such as walking or teleportation alone may not be good.
[59/S0/F4] The Majuli multimodal archive consists of regular images, 360-degree images, and videos for the indigenous architectural heritage of satras (Vaishnavite monasteries) and village life, 3D models for landscape and architecture, 2D drone shots for landscape, flora, and fauna, 2D videos for intangible cultural heritage, and textual descriptions.
[59/S0/F5] The Majuli virtual tour application is almost 10 gigabytes in size because the collected multimodal information is vast.
[59/S0/F6] Rendering the Majuli application's rich multimodal content, which can be interactively changed and includes videos and animations, involves a computational requirement many times higher than rendering a simple static image and requires fast storage and retrieval support for real-time realistic interactive rendering.
[59/S0/F7] Regular systems cannot support the Majuli application, so resource optimization was needed.
[59/S1/F0] The Oculus Quest 2 is a standalone computing device comprising a headset and handheld controllers, with its own processor and memory, and does not need to be tethered to a fixed computer.
[59/S1/F1] Compared with a high-end graphics workstation, the Oculus Quest 2 has less computing power and relatively less storage, so the Oculus Quest 2 application required optimization to reduce its size and associated computations, including memory access, because reducing the size reduces rendering requirements and the associated memory access.
[59/S1/F2] The Oculus Quest 2 application used a cloud computing-based optimization in which all videos, not images, were stored in the cloud because videos take up more space; when a user selected a video thumbnail from the Oculus Quest 2 video player, the video was loaded from the cloud rather than stored locally, reducing storage and data-access requirements.
[59/S1/F3] Because the videos were stored in the cloud, the application deployed on the Oculus Quest 2 client device was reduced to 5.1 gigabytes (GB), and a corresponding computational reduction occurred.
[59/S1/F4] The cloud-based video-loading optimization for the Oculus Quest 2 application relied on a cloud server, so it required high bandwidth support, network coverage, and minimal latency to load high-quality videos in real time; if these network connectivity issues were not resolved, the cloud-based optimization would not be of much help.
