[6/S0/F0] The historical evolution of virtual reality is broadly divided into four mostly distinct, sometimes overlapping phases: the early era, which existed before the advent of digital computers, the formative years from roughly 1950 to 1990, the modern phase from 1991 to 2015, and the current growth phase from roughly 2016 onwards.
[6/S1/F0] The early era in the historical evolution of virtual reality (VR) is the period before modern digital computers were developed in the 1940s, and although not everything was present in that era, a few key developments were conceptually similar to immersive virtual reality experiences.
[6/S1/F1] Sir Charles Wheatstone's 1838 demonstration of a stereoscopic device established, for the first time in recorded history, that the human brain can process two-dimensional (2D) images as three-dimensional (3D) objects, and this demonstration is roughly considered a first step toward immersive experience.
[6/S1/F2] In virtual reality (VR), headsets or display devices present two-dimensional (2D) images, but stereoscopic vision makes the user perceive them as three-dimensional (3D) objects and space, which is immersion.
[6/S1/F3] David Brewster's 1860 compact-sized stereoscope was an early form of stereoscopic display that created the illusion of depth in viewed images and, as an advancement over Sir Charles Wheatstone's 1838 stereoscopic device, is roughly considered another step toward practical immersive experiences.
[6/S1/F4] The flight simulator concept introduced for the first time in 1928 by Edwin A. Link evolved into sophisticated flight simulators with multiple sensors that provide an immersive experience, and this early development is considered a precursor to modern multi-sensory immersive virtual reality (VR).
[6/S2/F0] The formative years of virtual reality are the second phase of its historical evolution, roughly 1950 to 1990 after computers appeared in the 1940s, beginning with development of head-mounted displays (HMDs) in the 1950s and ending with popularization of the term virtual reality in the late 1980s.
[6/S2/F1] In the 1950s, especially toward the latter half of the decade, people started working on and considering different types of head-mounted displays (HMDs), a new kind of computer display that was less common than regular computer monitors.
[6/S2/F2] In 1961, engineers at Philco Corporation developed an early head-mounted display (HMD) that could track users' motion but was bulky and uncomfortable to wear.
[6/S2/F3] In 1962, Morton Heilig introduced Sensorama, which can be considered the first immersive virtual reality system under the modern definition of virtual reality.
[6/S2/F4] Sensorama was publicized as having 3D, wide vision, motion, color, stereo sound, smell, wind, and vibrations (haptics), and it stimulated all sensory organs by letting a user feel pre-recorded vibrations, sounds, smells, and wind.
[6/S2/F5] In 1965, Tom Furness and colleagues at the Wright-Patterson Air Force Base worked on visually coupled systems for pilots that consisted of head-mounted displays (HMDs).
[6/S2/F6] In 1967, Sutherland developed the Sword of Damocles, a head-mounted display (HMD), and was the first to demonstrate an HMD with head tracking and computer-generated imagery.
[6/S2/F7] In 1967, Fred Brooks at the University of North Carolina developed a haptic feedback system that used a force feedback device to let a user feel, through touch, objects shown on a screen while manipulating them.
[6/S2/F8] In 1973, Krueger used the term artificial reality, an early term that eventually evolved into the modern term virtual reality.
[6/S2/F9] In 1985, Scott Fisher and colleagues at the National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA) developed and launched Virtual Visual Environment Display (VIVED), the first commercially viable and commercially available stereoscopic head-tracked head-mounted display (HMD) with a wide field of view.
[6/S2/F10] In 1987, two papers appeared in Scientific American, one on the data glove, an input device, and these papers helped shape and popularize the field of virtual reality.
[6/S2/F11] In the late 1980s, Jaron Lanier proposed the term virtual reality, which became the term used to refer to immersive technologies.
[6/S3/F0] The modern phase of virtual reality (VR) is the period roughly from 1990 to 2015, about 25 years.
[6/S3/F1] The CAVE system, invented in 1992 at the University of Illinois at Chicago's Electronic Visualization Laboratory, used a single Silicon Graphics computer to render images in a surrounding setting that made a user feel surrounded by holograms and provided an immersive experience.
[6/S3/F2] In 1992, the company Sense8 developed the WTK software package for higher-level application development in virtual reality (VR), which helped in building virtual reality systems.
[6/S3/F3] Virtual reality modeling language (VRML) was presented in 1994, and other modeling languages for virtual reality (VR), such as X3D and Java 3D, also started in 1994.
[6/S3/F4] White 5 is a head-mounted display (HMD) with a 150-degree field of view (FOV) created in 2006 by Mark Bolas of the University of Southern California's MXR lab and Ian McDowall of Fakespace Labs.
[6/S3/F5] Head-mounted displays (HMDs) evolved from the bulky appearance of late-1950s models, through Sutherland's HMD and NASA HMDs, into a more portable, less bulky, and more usable form.
[6/S3/F6] Oculus Rift is an inexpensive virtual reality (VR) headset created by Palmer Luckey in 2012.
[6/S3/F7] Facebook purchased the Oculus Rift virtual reality (VR) headset in 2014, and with that purchase it tried to popularize the headset and made virtual reality affordable for the masses.
[6/S3/F8] The availability of inexpensive headsets, such as the Oculus Rift, greatly spurred interest in virtual reality (VR) towards the end of its modern phase.
[6/S4/F0] In the evolution of virtual reality (VR), the growth phase is the last and continuing phase and began roughly in 2016, when many well-known companies released commercial virtual reality (VR) headsets that made immersive experiences affordable to a large population and spurred growth in the field.
[6/S4/F1] The commercial virtual reality (VR) headsets released by well-known companies in 2016 include Oculus Rift version 1 from Facebook, HTC Vive from HTC, PlayStation virtual reality headset from Sony, and HoloLens from Microsoft.
[6/S4/F2] The term Metaverse had existed in the research community since the 1990s, but nobody paid much attention to the term; in 2021, Zuckerberg of Meta popularized the term, after which people began taking an interest in Metaverse and interpreting Metaverse in different ways, spurring further activity in the field.
[6/S4/F3] Apple released the Apple Vision Pro virtual reality (VR) headset in 2023.
[6/S4/F4] The virtual reality (VR) growth phase is a continuing phase because more developments, including better headsets, better immersive displays, and better input devices, will happen, will continue, and may accelerate because there is going to be huge demand.
[6/S5/F0] A book by LaValle is available online and covers the historical evolution of virtual reality (VR) in detail.
