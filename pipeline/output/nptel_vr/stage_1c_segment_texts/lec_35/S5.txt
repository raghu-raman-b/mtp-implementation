[35/132] Let us try to understand the importance of this method, From the point of view of system development.
[35/133] For example, let us recollect the calendar app that we have already talked about.
[35/134] Let us assume that the target group of users for the app are the managerial staff of any organization.
[35/135] Remember that we have to start by first identifying the group, the user group.
[35/136] So, in this case, let us assume that the user group comprises the managerial staff of a given organization.
[35/137] Now, without any knowledge about how the managerial staff behave in general when they are in their work setting, we can come up with all sorts of possible designs.
[35/138] So, we do not know what the unique behavioral traits of the managerial staff are unless we study them.
[35/139] And unless we study them, unless we gather the knowledge, we will not be able to refine the design.
[35/140] We will only be coming up with all sorts of design possibilities, although some of those or the majority of those may not be valid in the given work context.
[35/141] However, let us assume that we have performed a contextual inquiry and in the inquiry method, we observed Two important activities performed by the users, that is the managerial staff.
[35/142] Set meeting dates and reminders.
[35/143] Now, with this knowledge, we can be certain that these features must be included in the design.
[35/144] Otherwise, the system will not be usable for the given user group.
[35/145] So clearly, a calendar app intended to be used by this group of users must have support for these activities.
[35/146] Now if we had not done the contextual inquiry this knowledge would not have come to us and we probably would have come up with some design having all sorts of features where these two are more important but are given equal importance with other unnecessary features.
[35/147] So in that case, the usability would have been compromised.
[35/148] So, that is the importance of contextual inquiry.
