[11/49] Field of view is another VR content-related factor that affects our cyber sickness susceptibility.
[11/50] What is field of view?
[11/51] That is the extent to which the user's field of vision is captured by the display in a VR headset.
[11/52] Larger FOV means a larger field of vision is captured, and larger FOV generally results in greater immersion.
[11/53] Research has found that reducing the field of view can be an effective way to alleviate symptoms of cyber sickness.
[11/54] However, an excessively narrow field of view can lead to decreased immersion, which in turn leads to an increase in head movement to basically look for things in the environment, and that increase in head movement may be likely to exacerbate or increase CS.
[11/55] Of course, it is a challenge to find the optimal FOV, but that is very crucial for creating a comfortable VR experience.
[11/56] Type of content is another important factor.
[11/57] Action content, fast movements, and abrupt alterations in the VR environment are found to induce more cyber sickness as compared to neutral and static content.
[11/58] This somewhat is quite obvious.
[11/59] However, users with high preference for adrenaline sports, those who like to take part in adventurous things and high adrenaline sports, for them exposure to such content may not lead to cyber sickness.
[11/60] They are already accustomed to such behavior and such environment.
[11/61] So, for them, exposure to action content, fast movements, or abrupt alterations in the virtual environment may not lead to severe CS as compared to those who are not exposed to such types of sports or adventures.
[11/62] Genre of virtual environments also plays a significant role.
[11/63] Content horror- content based on horror scenes is likely to induce more sickness as compared to pleasant general.
[11/64] Geometry and terrain of VR also affect CS.
[11/65] So, when in a virtual world a user is traveling in the presence of regular bumps and irregular terrain, then in such scenario CS is likely to happen as compared to when the same travel is taking place with a flat terrain, on a flat terrain without bumpy rides.
[11/66] Weather conditions in the virtual environment: the kind of weather condition the user is exposed to can again affect the induction of CS.
[11/67] Higher CS is likely to happen during stormy conditions in the virtual environment as compared to clear weather conditions.
