[5/S0/F0] Augmented reality (AR) is defined by the Merriam-Webster dictionary as an enhanced version of reality created by the use of technology, primarily computer technology, to overlay digital information on an image of something being viewed through a device.
[5/S0/F1] The systemic definition of augmented reality (AR) states that an AR system should have three characteristics: combining the real world with virtual content, being interactive in real time so that interaction with the digital content added to the real-world view is reflected immediately, and being registered in 3D.
[5/S0/F2] An augmented reality (AR) system has four characteristics: it enhances the real world with computer-generated digital content such as image, video, or text; the enhancement is registered in 3D so that it appears at its appropriate place rather than randomly and mixes seamlessly with real-world objects; the enhancement is experienced through the user's sensory organs; and the system supports the user's interaction with the digital content in the enhanced environment rather than separately, giving the user the feel of being in reality at all times.
[5/S0/F3] A system is identified as an augmented reality (AR) system by checking the four AR characteristics one by one, and it is an AR system only if all four are satisfied; if at least one characteristic is not satisfied, it is not an AR system.
[5/S1/F0] Augmented reality (AR) alters the ongoing perception of the real world, whereas virtual reality (VR) completely replaces the user's sense of reality with a virtual environment.
[5/S1/F1] A virtual reality (VR) system does not require the physical existence of an object or place, because it creates a virtual environment, whereas an augmented reality (AR) or mixed reality (MR) system requires the place being experienced to exist.
[5/S1/F2] An augmented reality (AR) system can superimpose extra information, such as non-existent furniture, as digital content over the real-world view of a room.
[5/S2/F0] There is no agreement among practitioners and in the literature on the precise definition of mixed reality (MR).
[5/S2/F1] The dictionary definition of mixed reality (MR) describes MR as a medium consisting of immersive computer-generated environments in which elements of physical and virtual environments are combined, but this definition is not categorical about how MR differs from virtual reality (VR) or augmented reality (AR).
[5/S2/F2] One definition of mixed reality (MR) is that MR comprises everything in between the two extremes of the reality-virtuality continuum, the real environment and the virtual environment, so augmented reality (AR) is included in this broader definition of MR.
[5/S2/F3] Another definition of mixed reality (MR) is that MR denotes the capability to combine augmented reality (AR) and virtual reality (VR) technologies in the same application or on the same device, so such a combined application is called MR from a developer's point of view.
[5/S2/F4] Another definition of mixed reality (MR) describes MR as a strong version of augmented reality (AR), meaning a more capable version of augmented reality that has an advanced understanding of the physical environment and may be bound to specific hardware.
[5/S2/F5] In one definition used by many people, mixed reality (MR) is a synonym for augmented reality (AR), because in both cases the user views reality augmented or enhanced with digital content overlaid on the view of reality, and can interact with that content.
[5/S2/F6] Because most definitions of mixed reality (MR) are confusing and the controversy is not needed for this course, the course assumes that MR is the same technology as augmented reality (AR) and treats the two terms as interchangeable.
[5/S3/F0] In this course, mixed reality (MR) is assumed to be the same technology and idea as augmented reality (AR), a view shared by many if not all practitioners and adopted for simplicity in the course's subsequent discussion.
