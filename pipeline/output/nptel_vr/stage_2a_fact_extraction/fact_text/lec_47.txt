[47/S0/F0] The objective of the Majuli virtual reality (VR) tour case study is to capture tangible and intangible ecological and cultural heritage aspects of the Majuli river island and to digitally preserve that heritage information by providing users an immersive experience, because the island is disappearing.
[47/S0/F1] The evaluation study of the Majuli virtual reality (VR) tour aims to measure usability aspects of the system, specifically user engagement, presence, and comfort in the virtual environment, and to identify potential areas for improvement by analyzing participants' responses and symptoms of cyber sickness.

[47/S1/F0] The empirical evaluation study of the Majuli virtual reality (VR) tour used 27 volunteer participants, comprising 17 males and 10 females, who were divided into three groups of nine corresponding to the tour system's supported user categories of pilgrims, students, and tourists, with participants selected to have the characteristics of those categories.
[47/S1/F1] The participants in the empirical evaluation study of the Majuli virtual reality (VR) tour had ages between 23 and 40 years, with a mean age of 25.1 years and a standard deviation of 3.03 years.
[47/S1/F2] Among the 27 participants in the empirical evaluation study of the Majuli virtual reality (VR) tour, 21 had prior virtual reality experience, which may bias the evaluation output.
[47/S1/F3] All participants in the empirical evaluation study of the Majuli virtual reality (VR) tour were familiar with the joystick-based controller and with controller-based interaction, having used that type of controller in a virtual reality or non-virtual reality context.
[47/S1/F4] All participants in the empirical evaluation study of the Majuli virtual reality (VR) tour had normal or corrected-to-normal vision.
[47/S1/F5] In the empirical evaluation study of the Majuli virtual reality (VR) tour, the system was developed for the Meta Quest 2 head-mounted display (HMD), with a display resolution of 1920 by 1832 pixels per eye, and the HMD was connected to a personal computer (PC) via an enabled Oculus Link, the standard setup.
[47/S1/F6] In the empirical evaluation study of the Majuli virtual reality (VR) tour, the stimuli were rendered on a 64-bit computer running Windows 11 Professional operating system (OS), equipped with a 3.6 GHz quad-core processor and a 4GB GeForce GTX 1080 graphics processing unit (GPU).

[47/S2/F0] Presence is a psychological state of mind that is measured by standard questionnaires.
[47/S2/F1] The QEP questionnaire was used to measure presence, and it is a 2004 revised version of the standard presence questionnaire (PQ).
[47/S2/F2] The QEP questionnaire includes 19 core items and 3 optional items related to sound.
[47/S2/F3] Responses to the QEP questionnaire were recorded on a Likert scale ranging from 1 to 7.
[47/S2/F4] For the Majuli virtual reality (VR) tour assessment, the QEP questionnaire was tailored with two subjective items asking whether the user had previously visited the island in person and, if so, whether the virtual tour corresponded with the experience gained during the real visit.
[47/S2/F5] The simulator sickness questionnaire (SSQ) was used to measure cyber sickness, and it is a standard questionnaire that is popular for measuring cyber sickness in immersive experiences although it was not originally developed for cyber sickness.
[47/S2/F6] Participants rated their cyber sickness symptoms on the simulator sickness questionnaire (SSQ) using a scale from 0 to 3, where 0 means no symptoms and 3 means the most severe symptoms.
[47/S2/F7] The scores considered from the simulator sickness questionnaire (SSQ) were nausea, disorientation, oculomotor discomfort, and total sickness.
[47/S2/F8] The participants' simulator sickness questionnaire (SSQ) scores were computed before and after exposure and compared to get an idea of the effect of the Majuli virtual reality (VR) tour assessment design on those scores.
[47/S3/F0] In a virtual reality (VR) experiment, unfamiliar users may produce results much different from familiar users, so a brief pre-experiment training session that demonstrates the system's features and how to use them can bring participants to the same level and reduce or nullify familiarity or prior-experience bias, which otherwise may lead to incorrect conclusions.
[47/S3/F1] In the virtual reality (VR) tour evaluation, participants completed the simulator sickness questionnaire (SSQ) before exploration as a pre-exposure assessment, meaning before exposure to the system, completed the SSQ again after the experimental session as a post-exposure cybersickness measure, and completed the presence questionnaire (QEP) after exploration.
[47/S3/F2] In the virtual reality (VR) tour evaluation, each participant individually explored the entire tour in a controlled setting within 45 to 50 minutes.
[47/S3/F3] The 45 to 50 minute exploration time was estimated from pilot studies, trials, and literature to give users sufficient time to explore the large island and obtain heritage information while minimizing the possibility of cybersickness, which depends on exposure.

[47/S4/F0] The presence measure used to evaluate the virtual tour of Majuli Island included involvement, interface quality, adaptation or immersion, and visual fidelity, and tangible and intangible reconstruction fidelity was additionally reported for participants who had previously visited Majuli Island.
[47/S4/F1] The overall user presence ratings for the virtual tour of Majuli Island were involvement mean 5.65 (standard deviation 1.11, coefficient of variation 0.19), interface quality mean 4.37 (standard deviation 1.4, coefficient of variation 0.32), adaptation and immersion mean 5.4 (standard deviation 1.13, coefficient of variation 0.2), and visual fidelity mean 5.79 (standard deviation 0.93, coefficient of variation 0.16).
[47/S4/F2] The presence ratings for participants who had previously visited Majuli Island were tangible and intangible reconstruction fidelity mean 5.68 (standard deviation 1, coefficient of variation 0.177), involvement mean 5.83 (standard deviation 1.05, coefficient of variation 0.18), interface quality mean 4.72 (standard deviation 1.36, coefficient of variation 0.33), adaptation or immersion mean 5.61 (standard deviation 1.15, coefficient of variation 0.22), and visual fidelity mean 5.94 (standard deviation 0.93, coefficient of variation 0.15).
[47/S4/F3] The overall presence results for the virtual tour of Majuli Island were summarized as good presence, meaning that users felt reasonably well located within the virtual environment while virtually exploring Majuli Island.
[47/S4/F4] Previous visitors to Majuli Island's more favorable opinions for all factors, despite their higher expectations, indicated that they felt more comfortable with the digital exploration of the island and that the system reasonably well mimicked the physical island environment in its design elements, interaction, and overall implementation, and that the digital avatar was close to reality.
[47/S4/F5] A statistically significant difference between users with prior exposure to digital or virtual tours and users without such exposure was revealed by a Welch t-test, supporting the conclusion that prior exposure to such systems always helps users gain a better experience.
[47/S5/F0] The cyber sickness evaluation of the Majuli virtual tour used the SSQ questionnaire and collected pre-exposure and post-exposure data for nausea, disorientation, oculomotor effects, and total severity.
[47/S5/F1] The box plots of average pre- and post-exposure SSQ data for the Majuli virtual tour showed little difference for nausea and disorientation, indicating that the Majuli virtual tour system did not generally induce cyber sickness, although total severity and oculomotor severity showed some difference.
[47/S5/F2] The statistical significance test performed on the pre- and post-exposure SSQ data for the Majuli virtual tour was a Wilcoxon signed-rank test, and it revealed a statistically significant difference between pre- and post-exposure outcomes for the oculomotor item and the total sickness item.
[47/S5/F3] The statistically significant pre- to post-exposure change in total sickness in the Majuli virtual tour indicates that the Majuli virtual tour design had some effect on total sickness.
