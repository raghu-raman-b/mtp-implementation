[33/S0/F1] The Majuli case study is about the digital preservation of the physical heritage of the physical space of the Majuli River Island, focusing on the design, implementation, and evaluation of an extended reality system, specifically a virtual reality system that digitally preserves the ecological and cultural heritage of the Majuli River Island.
[33/S1/F0] Majuli River Island is a river island on the Brahmaputra River in the eastern part of Assam, Northeast India, and it is considered the world's largest inhabited river island and a potential United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization (UNESCO) World Heritage Site contender.
[33/S1/F1] Majuli's monsoon season is typically May to September, its non-monsoon season is October to April, and it has an average annual precipitation of 1704 millimeters, an average maximum temperature of 33 degrees Celsius, and a minimum temperature of 7 degrees Celsius.
[33/S1/F2] Majuli's area was about 1250 square kilometers around 1900, had reduced to about 570 square kilometers in 1975, and had further reduced to about 427 square kilometers by 2021, with 2021 data giving about 427.09 square kilometers, an overall reduction of almost two-thirds and about a 25 percent reduction from 1975 to 2021.
[33/S1/F3] Majuli has been constantly hit by severe annual floods and erosion, and river erosion is consuming the island and reducing its habitable area, so formerly habitable areas are no longer habitable and have become sandbars, river banks, underwater areas, or barren areas.
[33/S1/F4] The severe annual floods and erosion of Majuli endanger its cultural resources and its material existence, and although measures are being taken to prevent further erosion, there is a real possibility that the island may disappear.
[33/S1/F5] Satras are Vaishnavite monasteries on Majuli that are authoritative centers of Vaishnavite faith in Assam and are living workshops of different kinds of visual and performing arts.
[33/S1/F6] Majuli is home to several tribal communities, including three distinctive tribal communities—Mising, Deori, and Sonowal Kachari—whose distinctive ways of living, rituals, heritage, and culture make them unique.
[33/S1/F7] Tangible heritage of Majuli consists of heritage aspects that someone can touch and feel, such as cultural artifacts like masks and musical instruments and the architectural aspects of Satras and traditional tribal homes.
[33/S1/F8] Intangible heritage of Majuli consists of heritage aspects that cannot be touched and felt but can be sensed, including traditional skills, knowledge, and aesthetic norms in music, dance forms, drama or performing arts, mask making, vernacular architecture, weaving, food, and agricultural practices, and it is typically embedded in the daily and occasional activities of social life.
[33/S1/F9] Majuli also has ecological heritage, consisting of the flora and fauna that exist because of the island's unique geology, geography, and topography from natural formation.
[33/S1/F10] Before Majuli disappears, it is essential to preserve its tangible heritage, intangible heritage, and ecological heritage.
[33/S2/F0] Traditional methods for preserving cultural heritage include oral traditions, museums, maintenance of historical sites, and documentation.
[33/S2/F1] Digital preservation is defined by UNESCO as the use of digital media to preserve educational, scientific, and other resources.
[33/S2/F2] Digital heritage preservation is the process of using digital media to preserve heritage, whether cultural, educational, scientific, ecological, or other, and it involves archiving, digitizing, and using immersive technologies such as virtual reality (VR) and augmented reality (AR).
[33/S2/F3] In digital heritage preservation, archiving means storing visual and audio files that capture the heritage information of a particular entity, object, space, or place.
[33/S2/F4] In digital heritage preservation, immersive technologies can recreate a physical space as a virtual world, such as a virtual tour, and, if properly designed, allow users to interact with entities in the virtual world, making the experience more engaging than accessing information through a computer screen.
[33/S2/F5] Creating digital avatars of the elements and objects of a physical space can preserve fragile artifacts that may not survive the passage of time.
[33/S2/F6] Virtual reality (VR) and other immersive technologies permit a global audience to explore heritage sites, artifacts, and intangible aspects of culture remotely from home without visiting the physical site.
[33/S2/F7] The use of virtual reality (VR) and other immersive technologies in heritage preservation is known to play a pivotal role in promoting the preservation of heritage for the benefit of future generations.
[33/S3/F0] The virtual reality (VR) system for Majuli River Island has two clear-cut goals: to preserve heritage and to let users anywhere on Earth experience that heritage.
[33/S3/F1] Heritage preservation in the virtual reality (VR) system for Majuli River Island means creating an archive of heritage information that is ideally a multimedia archive comprising images, videos, audio, and text, with images and videos in two-dimensional (2D) and 360-degree forms.
[33/S3/F2] Experiencing heritage in the virtual reality (VR) system for Majuli River Island is intended as an immersive virtual tour or exploration of Majuli River Island that uses the archive of heritage information.
