  Virtual means something that is there in essence or effect but not in fact, while reality is something that exists independently of ideas.   - Virtual means something that is there in essence or effect but not in fact.
  - Reality is something that exists independently of ideas.
  - Virtual reality is a computer-provided artificial environment where user actions partially determine what happens.

  - A virtual reality system is defined by an artificial environment, sensory experience, and user interaction.
  - An artificial environment is a computer-generated representation of a real or imaginary world.
  - Sensory experience involves computer-generated stimuli to provide an immersive experience.
  - User interaction allows users to act on elements to produce dynamic changes in the environment.

===== lecture 6: Historical Evolution

6/A0  recap  6/S0  "Recap"  (1 facts, 6 concepts)
  The historical evolution of virtual reality is broadly divided into four mostly distinct, sometimes overlapping phases. These include the early era before the advent of digital computers, the formative years from roughly 1950 to 1990, the modern phase from 1991 to 2015, and the current growth phase from roughly 2016 onwards.
  - The historical evolution of virtual reality is broadly divided into four mostly distinct, sometimes overlapping phases.
  - The phases are the early era, formative years (roughly 1950 to 1990), the modern phase (1991 to 2015), and the current growth phase (roughly 2016 onwards).

6/A1  recap  6/S1  "Recap"  (5 facts, 20 concepts)
  The early era of virtual reality occurred before modern digital computers and featured key developments like Sir Charles Wheatstone's 1838 stereoscopic device. This device proved that the human brain can process 2D images as 3D objects, which is how modern headsets create immersion. Further developments, such as David Brewster's 1860 compact stereoscope and Edwin A. Link's 1928 flight simulator, served as precursors to modern multi-sensory immersive experiences.
  - The early era of virtual reality occurred before modern digital computers were developed in the 1940s.
  - Sir Charles Wheatstone's 1838 stereoscopic device demonstrated that the human brain can process 2D images as 3D objects.
  - Stereoscopic vision is the mechanism where headsets present 2D images that the brain perceives as 3D objects and space.
  - David Brewster's 1860 compact stereoscope was an advancement toward practical immersive experiences.
  - Edwin A. Link's 1928 flight simulator was a precursor to modern multi-sensory immersive virtual reality.

6/A2  recap  6/S2  "Recap"  (11 facts, 35 concepts)
  The formative years of virtual reality began in the 1950s with the development of head-mounted displays (HMDs). These technologies progressed into immersive systems featuring haptic feedback and the first commercially viable stereoscopic head-tracked HMD. By the late 1980s, the terminology shifted from "artificial reality" to the modern term "virtual reality."
  - The formative years of virtual reality began in the 1950s with the development of head-mounted displays (HMDs).
  - Immersive systems and haptic feedback technologies were introduced in the 1960s.
  - The first commercially viable stereoscopic head-tracked HMD was launched in 1985.
  - The term "virtual reality" was proposed in the late 1980s as an evolution of "artificial reality."

6/A3  recap  6/S3  "Recap"  (9 facts, 23 concepts)
  The modern phase of virtual reality lasted from approximately 1990 to 2015, during which systems like CAVE and software tools like WTK were developed. Modeling languages were introduced in 1994, while head-mounted displays evolved into more portable and usable designs. The availability of affordable headsets like the Oculus Rift eventually made virtual reality accessible to the masses.
  - The modern phase of virtual reality lasted from approximately 1990 to 2015.
  - New systems like CAVE and software tools like WTK were developed in the early 1990s.
  - Modeling languages such as VRML, X3D, and Java 3D were introduced in 1994.
  - Head-mounted displays evolved from bulky models into more portable and usable designs.
  - Affordable headsets like the Oculus Rift made virtual reality accessible to the masses.

6/A4  recap  6/S4  "Recap"  (3 facts, 9 concepts)
  The growth phase of VR began roughly in 2016 when commercial headsets made immersive experiences affordable. The popularization of the Metaverse term in 2021 led to increased interest and activity in the field. This growth phase is continuing and may accelerate because of huge demand for better technology.
  - The growth phase of VR began roughly in 2016 when commercial headsets made immersive experiences affordable.
  - The popularization of the Metaverse term in 2021 led to increased interest and activity in the field.
  - The growth phase is a continuing phase that may accelerate because of huge demand for better technology.

===== lecture 7: Historial Evolution

7/A0  recap  7/S0  "Recap"  (2 facts, 12 concepts)
  The historical evolution of augmented reality can be broadly divided into four overlapping phases from before the 1980s to the present. These phases progressed from early experimentation and basic research into the development of tools and widespread commercial applications.
  - The historical evolution of augmented reality can be broadly divided into four overlapping phases from before the 1980s to the present.
  - These phases progressed from early experimentation and basic research into the development of tools and widespread commercial applications.

7/A1  recap  7/S1  "Recap"  (5 facts, 21 concepts)
  Ivan Sutherland and Bob Sproull created the first computer-generated augmented reality prototype in 1968. This prototype used a head-mounted display and a tracking system to register head movements in three dimensions. The US Air Force super cockpit program produced technologies where pilots saw computer-generated imagery through a head-mounted display. These early experiments led to modern aircraft displays, virtual reality systems, and tracking hardware.
  - Ivan Sutherland and Bob Sproull created the first computer-generated augmented reality prototype in 1968.
  - This prototype used a head-mounted display and a tracking system to register head movements in three dimensions.
  - The US Air Force super cockpit program produced technologies where pilots saw computer-generated imagery through a head-mounted display.
  - These early experiments led to modern aircraft displays, virtual reality systems, and tracking hardware.

7/A2  recap  7/S2  "Recap"  (8 facts, 17 concepts)
  The basic research phase of augmented reality came after the experimentation phase and shaped the field during the 1990s, including the coining of the term "augmented reality" in 1992. Research during this phase focused on tracking techniques, head-mounted displays, and interaction methods. Early systems like Chameleon and NaviCam were true augmented reality applications and precursors to the kind of AR applications seen today.
  - The basic research phase of augmented reality came after the experimentation phase and shaped the field during the 1990s.
  - Tom Caudell coined the term "augmented reality" in 1992.
  - Research during this phase focused on tracking techniques, head-mounted displays, and interaction methods.
  - Early systems like Chameleon and NaviCam were true augmented reality applications and precursors to the kind of AR applications seen today.

7/A3  recap  7/S3  "Recap"  (12 facts, 42 concepts)
  Research in the mid-1990s focused on key technologies like tracking, display, and interaction, leading to developments in shared AR content, medical applications, and wearable computing. By 1997, computer vision and GPS were used to register 3D content in the real world. In the early 2000s, wearable AR systems were developed for various applications but remained bulky and difficult to use. Viewpoint tracking challenges were eventually addressed by systems like the AR Toolkit, which was later ported to mobile phones in 2004.
  - Mid-1990s research focused on key enabling technologies like tracking, display, and interaction.
  - This period saw the development of shared AR content, medical applications, and wearable computing using head-mounted displays.
  - By 1997, tracking techniques using computer vision markers and GPS were used to register 3D content.
  - In the early 2000s, wearable AR systems were developed for various applications but were bulky and difficult to use.
  - Viewpoint tracking was addressed by tools such as the AR Toolkit, which was later ported to mobile phones in 2004.

7/A4  recap  7/S4  "Recap"  (5 facts, 24 concepts)
  The commercial application phase of augmented reality began in October 2007 and moved from research laboratories to widely available technology by 2009, driven by new authoring tools, smartphones, and marketing campaigns. The launch of ARKit and ARCore in 2017 further stimulated growth by enabling developers to create complex applications. This phase is expected to continue growing as hardware improves and AI and computer vision technologies advance.
  - The commercial application phase of augmented reality began in October 2007 and made the technology popular among consumers.
  - In 2009, augmented reality moved from research laboratories to widely available commercial technology.
  - This shift was driven by Adobe Flash-based authoring tools, the start of the smartphone era, and global marketing campaigns.
  - The launch of ARKit and ARCore in 2017 stimulated growth by enabling developers to create more complex applications.
  - The commercial application phase is still ongoing and is expected to continue growing through improvements in hardware, AI, and computer vision.

===== lecture 8: Issues & Challenges

8/A0  recap  8/S0  "Recap"  (3 facts, 8 concepts)
  The main objective of a virtual reality system is to let users experience artificial environments through sensory stimuli provided for all sensory organs. VR involves technological issues in generating stimuli and psychological issues in how users perceive them. These issues give rise to the two major concerns of immersion and presence.
  - VR aims to let users experience artificial environments through sensory stimuli for all sensory organs.
  - VR involves technological issues in generating stimuli and psychological issues in how humans perceive them.
  - These issues give rise to the two major concerns of immersion and presence.

8/A1  recap  8/S1  "Recap"  (8 facts, 17 concepts)
