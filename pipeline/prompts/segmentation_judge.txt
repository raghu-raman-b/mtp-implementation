WHAT COUNTS AS A DEPENDENCY
- The segment depends on the earlier part of the lecture when it needs something that only the earlier part explains and that the segment itself never names or restates, so that a learner starting here cannot follow its main point. Examples: "these three values" when the values are not given again; "this algorithm" when the algorithm is never named; "the R3 question" when the questions R1 to R3 are defined only in the earlier part.
- These are not dependencies:
  - connectives and transitions: "So,", "Now,", "Given this background,", or "This brings us to ..." followed by what it brings us to;
  - a short recap, or a reference to something earlier that the segment names ("Head-mounted displays, which we discussed earlier, ...");
  - anything taught in earlier lectures, or known to a student with this course's prerequisites;
  - a word like "this" or "it" whose meaning the segment itself makes clear, in that sentence or the ones right after it.
- Judge the segment's main point. A passing mention the learner can do without is not a dependency.
- A dependency must be explained in the earlier part shown below, and you must give the sentence where. If the segment refers to something the earlier part does not explain (an example or a question from an earlier lecture, say), the learner already knows it from that lecture: it is not a dependency.
