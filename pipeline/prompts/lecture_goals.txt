TASK
You write the goals of one lecture of a university course, from the sub-goals of its segments. The lecture was divided into segments, and each segment achieves one sub-goal: something the learner can do after it. Build the two levels above the sub-goals:
1. Objectives. Group the segments into the larger steps the lecture takes. An objective is what the learner can do after a run of consecutive segments that work toward the same thing.
2. The lecture goal. What the learner can do after the whole lecture.
The goals are used to tell a learner what a lecture is for, to answer what a lecture or a part of it teaches, and to build the goals of the whole course from the goals of its lectures.

OBJECTIVES
- An objective covers one or more consecutive segments. Every segment belongs to exactly one objective, except a segment that only summarises or closes the lecture, which belongs to none.
- Group segments when their sub-goals are steps toward one thing (defining something and then applying the definition; the parts of one system, one after the other). Keep segments apart when each could be a lecture topic of its own. A lecture usually has one to four objectives; a short lecture may have one.
- An objective is not a list of its sub-goals joined with "and". It names the one thing they add up to.

EVERY GOAL
- "goal": one sentence that says what the learner can do, starting with a verb ("Explain why ...", "Classify ...", "Describe how ..."). It names the subject matter itself, not "the lecture" or "the topics".
- "achieves": one sentence that says what reaching the goal gives the learner in this course: what it lets them understand or do next, or which aim of the course it serves. Take this from the course description above and the sub-goals; do not promise what neither of them supports.
- Claim only what the sub-goals cover. Do not add an ability that no segment teaches.

ANSWER

"objectives": a list in lecture order, each with "segments" (the numbers of its segments), "goal" and "achieves".
"goal": the lecture goal.
