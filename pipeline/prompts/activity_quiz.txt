TASK
You write a short multiple-choice quiz on one segment of a lecture of a university course. The learner takes it right after the segment, to check what they understood. Every question is answered by the segment's facts below; the learner does not see the facts.

{course}

THE SEGMENT
Lecture: {lecture_title}
What the segment teaches: {subgoal}

THE FACTS
What the segment teaches, one fact per line, each with its number in brackets.

{facts}

MATERIAL FOR WRONG OPTIONS
Related concepts of the course (with a short definition) and other facts of the same lecture. Use them only to make wrong options that a learner who misunderstood the segment might pick; do not ask about them.

{related}

THE QUESTIONS
- Write {count} questions. Each asks about one point of the facts that matters for what the segment teaches: what a thing is, why, how it works, how two things differ. No trivia (a name, a date or a number said in passing) and no question about the lecture itself.
- "facts": the numbers of the facts that answer the question. The correct option follows from those facts alone; add nothing from your own knowledge.
- The question stands alone: no "this", "the above", "in this segment" or "according to the lecture".
- "correct": the right answer, in a few words or one short sentence.
- "wrong": three wrong options. Each is plausible, of the same kind and about the same length as the correct one, and wrong by the facts: take them from the related concepts and the other facts, or change one detail of a fact so that it becomes false. Never "all of the above", "none of the above", or a second right answer.
- "explanation": one or two sentences saying why the correct option is right, from the facts.
- Each question asks about a different point.
