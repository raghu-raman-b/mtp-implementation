You list the concepts in the facts of one segment of a university lecture.

The facts below were extracted from one segment of a lecture transcript and
checked against it. A concept is a term of the course subject: a thing the
subject has a name for, such as a technology, device, component, method,
process, quantity, property, principle, or a named system, standard or model.
It is what a student would look up in the glossary or the index of a textbook
on the subject, and what would be a node in a concept map of the course. The
concepts are used to find the facts about a term when a student asks about
it, to link the lectures that teach or use the same term, and to build
concept maps and exercises.

Requirements:
1. From the facts only. Take every concept the facts name. Do not add a
   concept the facts do not name, even if it belongs to the topic.
2. Terms of the subject, not ordinary words. Take a term of the subject even
   when the facts only mention it while teaching something else. Leave out
   ordinary words and descriptions that the facts only use to say something
   ("the real world", "the user", "a problem"). Leave out a source that is
   only named as where a statement comes from (a dictionary, a report, a
   survey). A named product, system, person or organisation is a concept
   only when a fact says something about it that belongs to the subject.
3. One concept per thing. If the facts use several names or wordings for the
   same thing ("headset", "head-mounted display", "HMD"), that is one
   concept. A thing and a kind of it, a thing and a part of it, and a thing
   and a property of it are different concepts ("display" and "head-mounted
   display"; "tracking" and "tracking accuracy").
4. Name. Give the concept the name a textbook glossary would use: the full
   term in words, singular, lower case except for proper names, no article
   ("head-mounted display", not "HMDs" or "the headset"). The name is never
   an acronym or abbreviation and never contains one: "virtual reality
   headset", not "VR headset"; "three-dimensional space", not "3D space".
   When a fact gives the full term with its acronym in brackets ("field of
   view (FoV)", "three-dimensional (3D) space"), the name is the full term.
   When the facts write only the acronym, still name the concept by the
   full term the acronym stands for, and list the acronym as an alias. The
   name of a product or company is kept as it is written ("Microsoft
   HoloLens").
5. Aliases. List every other name the facts use for the concept, written as
   in the facts: its acronym or abbreviation ("XR") and other wordings
   ("headset"). When part of the name is abbreviated in the facts, the
   abbreviated wording is an alias ("3D space" for "three-dimensional
   space"). Acronyms belong here and only here. Do not add an alias the
   facts do not use. Most concepts have one or none.
6. Facts. Give the numbers of all the facts that name the concept: the facts
   that say something about it, and the facts that use it to say something
   about another concept. Every concept has at least one fact, and every
   fact should belong to at least one concept.
7. Defined. true when the facts of this segment teach what the concept is:
   one of them defines it, or says what it consists of or how it works, so
   that a student who read these facts could answer "what is it?". false
   when the facts only mention it: they use it to teach something else, or
   say something about it without saying what it is.

List the concepts in the order the facts first name them. Output JSON only:
{"concepts": [{"name": "...", "aliases": ["..."], "facts": [int, ...], "defined": true}]}

### Example 1
COURSE TITLE: Virtual Reality
LECTURE: Introduction to immersive technologies
SEGMENT GOAL: Distinguish XR and AR from VR
FACTS:
[1] Extended reality (XR) is an umbrella term, not one device or product, that covers virtual reality (VR), augmented reality (AR), mixed reality (MR) and future technologies of the same kind.
[2] Augmented reality (AR) lets the user still see the real world, with virtual content overlaid on top of it.
OUTPUT:
{"concepts": [
{"name": "extended reality", "aliases": ["XR"], "facts": [1], "defined": true},
{"name": "virtual reality", "aliases": ["VR"], "facts": [1], "defined": false},
{"name": "augmented reality", "aliases": ["AR"], "facts": [1, 2], "defined": true},
{"name": "mixed reality", "aliases": ["MR"], "facts": [1], "defined": false}
]}
Virtual reality and mixed reality are only named as members of extended reality, so they are not defined here. "Umbrella term", "the real world" and "virtual content" are ordinary words that the facts use to describe the concepts.

### Example 2
COURSE TITLE: Data Structures
LECTURE: Hashing
SEGMENT GOAL: Explain what a hash table is and what its operations cost
FACTS:
[1] A hash table stores key-value pairs, finds the slot for a key by applying a hash function to the key, and is not ordered, so its keys cannot be traversed in key order.
[2] Lookup and insertion in a hash table each take constant time on average and linear time in the worst case, when every key lands in the same slot.
[3] A collision in a hash table is two keys landing in the same slot.
OUTPUT:
{"concepts": [
{"name": "hash table", "aliases": [], "facts": [1, 2, 3], "defined": true},
{"name": "key-value pair", "aliases": [], "facts": [1], "defined": false},
{"name": "slot", "aliases": [], "facts": [1, 2, 3], "defined": false},
{"name": "hash function", "aliases": [], "facts": [1], "defined": false},
{"name": "lookup", "aliases": [], "facts": [2], "defined": false},
{"name": "insertion", "aliases": [], "facts": [2], "defined": false},
{"name": "constant time", "aliases": [], "facts": [2], "defined": false},
{"name": "linear time", "aliases": [], "facts": [2], "defined": false},
{"name": "collision", "aliases": [], "facts": [3], "defined": true}
]}
The hash function is used to say how a hash table works; the two operations and the two costs are named without being explained; a slot is named in all three facts but never explained. None of them is defined here. "Key order" is a description, not a term.
