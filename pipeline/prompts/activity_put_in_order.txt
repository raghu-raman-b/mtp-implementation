THE EXERCISES
- Most lectures have none or one. Write an exercise only where the facts state an order: the steps of a procedure, the stages of a pipeline from input to output, the path something travels, events in time, phases that follow one another. The facts must say what comes after what ("first", "then", "after", "before", "in the order", numbered stages, dates). The order is never your own knowledge, and never only the order in which a fact happens to list things.
- Test every pair of neighbouring steps: could the two be swapped without contradicting a fact? Then the facts do not fix their order: leave one of the two out, or drop the exercise.
- These are not sequences, so write no exercise from them: the kinds, parts, factors or properties of a thing; a problem with its cause, its solution and what the solution needs or brings; the things done in a project when the facts do not say which came after which; choices that can be made in any order.
- No exercise that a learner could solve without the lecture, from the wording alone ("the task is started", "the task is executed", "the task is completed"; "input is given", "it is processed", "output is produced"). Prefer four or more steps; write three only when their order is a point the lecture makes.
- A scale, a range or a stack of layers has two ends: use it only when the title says at which end to start ("from the lowest layer up", "from the coldest to the hottest").
- If the facts give no such order, answer with an empty list: no exercise is better than a forced one.
- "title": what is put in order, as a short instruction that stands alone and names the process in full ("Order the stages of the water cycle"). It does not reveal the order and gives no count ("the first four stages").
- "steps": three to eight steps in the correct order.
  - Each step says in a few words what happens in it or what is passed through. Where the facts say what a stage does, describe the stage rather than only naming it: ordering descriptions takes more understanding than ordering names.
  - All steps have the same grammatical form and about the same length, and each is one distinct moment: one event is not split into two steps, and two events are not put into one.
  - Nothing in the wording sorts the steps by itself: no "first", "then", "next", "finally", "initial", "final", no step numbers, dates or years, and no step that speaks of "the previous step".
- "facts": the numbers of the facts that give the steps and their order.
