TASK
You write the goals of a university course from the goals of its lectures. A course goal is one of the few large things a learner can do after the whole course. Each course goal is reached through several lectures. The course goals are the top of a hierarchy (course goal, lecture goal, objective, segment sub-goal) that is used to tell a learner what the course is for, which lectures each aim needs, and where a lecture fits.

{course}

THE LECTURES
Each lecture is given with its number, its title, its goal, and its objectives.

{lectures}

COURSE GOALS
- Write as many course goals as the course has distinct aims, usually four to eight. Two goals that most lectures would serve together are one goal; a goal that needs half the course is probably two.
- "goal": one sentence that says what the learner can do, starting with a verb. It names the subject matter itself, not "the course" or "the topics".
- "achieves": one sentence that says what reaching the goal gives the learner: what it lets them understand, build or decide.
- "lectures": the numbers of the lectures whose goals work toward this course goal, in course order. Every lecture serves at least one course goal. A lecture may serve more than one when its objectives belong to different aims. The lectures of a goal need not be consecutive.
- Build the goals from the lecture goals and objectives given. Use the course description above to see how the course is meant to be organised, but do not write a goal that no lecture serves.

ANSWER
Answer with a JSON object with two fields, in this order:
"reasoning": in a few sentences, the aims you found in the lectures and why you divided them this way.
"goals": a list of course goals, each with "lectures", "goal" and "achieves", in this order.
Answer with the JSON only.
