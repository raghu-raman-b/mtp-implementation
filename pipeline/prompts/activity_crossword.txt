TASK
You write the clues of a crossword on one lecture of a university course. Each answer is a concept of the lecture. The learner sees the grid, the clues and the number of letters of each answer, and nothing else; solving the crossword is a way to recall the concepts. So each clue must lead a learner who knows the lecture to its answer, and to no other answer.

{lecture_title}

THE ANSWERS
Each answer with its number in brackets, its other names and its definition (or the lecture's main statement about it).

{answers}

THE CLUES
- One clue per answer, by its number.
- What a clue says. What the concept is or does: the property that makes it this concept and not another one. Take it from the text given for the answer and add nothing from your own knowledge. When that text is mostly about another thing, use only what it says about the answer itself, and check that the clue describes the answer and not that other thing.
- Not where the answer stands in a list, and not a figure: "the second of the four steps", "one of the three states of matter" and "about 4% of the market" do not say what the thing is. When the text only names the answer in a list, say what the list is a list of and what sets this member apart, putting the plain meaning of its name into other words ("the layer of the Earth that lies deepest" for "inner core").
- Its own answer only. Read each clue against every other answer in the list: no two clues could be swapped, so sibling concepts each get the detail that tells them apart. A clue also fails when it fits a more familiar thing better than its answer.
- It stands alone. The learner reads the clue without the text: name the setting it needs ("in a compiler", "in the nitrogen cycle"), and no "their", "these", "the above", "the software" or "the sequence" that points at something the clue does not name.
- Form: a phrase that could stand in the place of the answer (for a noun, a noun phrase: "Study of how physical stimuli are perceived", not "When stimuli are studied"), of at most fifteen words, without a full stop at the end.
- Never the answer's words: not the answer, not any of its other names, not any word of them in any form (not "table" or "tables" for "hash table"), and not a longer word built on one of them ("graphical" for "graph"). Say it in other words.
- No "this lecture" or "the lecturer".
