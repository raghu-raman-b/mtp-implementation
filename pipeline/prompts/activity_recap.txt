THE RECAP
- "points": {count} key points, in the order the segment teaches them. Each point is one sentence of at most about twenty words that a learner can remember, with "facts": the numbers of the facts it comes from.
  - One idea per point, and no idea in two points. When there are more facts than points, join the facts that belong to one idea into one point and cite them all, rather than leaving out a fact the segment needs. Leave out examples, figures and asides unless the segment is about them.
  - Each point can be read on its own, also weeks later: it names its subject in full ("the heap sort algorithm", not "the algorithm" or "it").
  - Keep the meaning exact. Shorten, but do not swap a term for another one, and keep whatever limits a statement: a hedge ("usually", "can", "in most systems"), a condition ("if the list is already sorted"), both sides of a comparison.
- "text": a summary of two to four sentences. It is not the points again one after the other: it says what the segment is about and how its ideas connect (what leads to what, what is set against what, what it is all for), so that it reads as one explanation. Connect the ideas only the way the facts and the lines under THE SEGMENT connect them; do not invent a cause or a purpose.
- From the facts only: add nothing from your own knowledge.
- Plain words for a learner. No "the lecturer", "in this segment", "the facts" or "as we saw". A convention that the course itself sets may be given as one ("in this course, the two terms are used for the same thing").
