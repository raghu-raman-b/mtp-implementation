The learner answers each question in a few sentences of their own after the lecture; the answer is compared with the reference answer, and the learner then reads the reference answer as feedback. The learner never sees the facts. A good open question asks the learner to explain, compare or reason, and its reference answer is the answer a learner who understood the lecture would give. Decide for each question whether it meets all seven criteria below. A question that fails is rewritten once and then dropped.

THE SEVEN CRITERIA
1. answerable. Every statement of the reference answer is stated by the key facts the question cites: nothing from outside, nothing from a fact that is not cited (give its number), no hedge dropped ("can", "usually"), nothing made stronger. A learner who knows only those facts could write this answer.
2. answers_question. The reference answer answers what is asked, every part of it, and says so directly. It fails when it sets related facts side by side without stating the link the question asks for: asked "how does A improve on B", it describes A and describes B but never says how. It fails too when the facts hold no such link, so that no answer from the facts is possible.
3. open. Answering takes putting two or more facts together: a reason, a comparison, a consequence, how a part serves the whole. It fails for a question that only asks to repeat one fact, a list or a figure. It fails for a forced link: a question that ties two things together only because they share a term, or ties this lecture to an earlier one that it does not in fact build on.
4. one_thread. The question asks one thing, and the reference answer is the answer to it: a learner who understood the lecture would give an answer that can be compared with the reference. It fails for two questions joined into one ("How ..., and why ...?") and for a question so wide that many different answers would be as good.
5. no_giveaway. The question does not contain its own answer or most of it.
6. clear. The question is one or two grammatical sentences that stand alone (no "this", "the above"; things named in full) and presuppose nothing odd. The reference answer is two to four plain sentences of about eighty words at most, with the direct answer first.
7. no_source_talk. Neither the question nor the reference answer speaks of the lecturer, the lecture, the video or the facts ("in this lecture", "according to the lecture"). A convention the course itself sets, stated as such, is not source talk.

HOW TO CHECK
For each question: read the question only and the facts it cites, and write the direct answer in one sentence as those facts give it; if they give none, say so. Then read the reference answer: compare it with your sentence, and put each of its statements next to the key fact that states it. Then go through the criteria. Fail a criterion only for a problem you can point to and quote. When you are still in doubt: on criteria 1 and 2 fail the question; on the others pass it.

ANSWER
Answer with a JSON object with one field, "verdicts": a list with one entry per question, in the order given, each with these fields in this order:

"pass": true if "failed" is empty, otherwise false.
