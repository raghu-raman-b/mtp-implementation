TASK
You write the glossary definition of one concept of a university course, from the facts that the lectures teach about it. The definition is what a student sees when they look the concept up, and it is the one definition of the concept for the whole course.

{course}

THE CONCEPT
Name: {name}
Other names: {aliases}

THE FACTS
The facts from the course that name the concept, in lecture order, each with its number in brackets and the lecture it comes from. Facts marked * come from a segment that defines the concept.

{facts}

THE DEFINITION
- It says what the concept is. Begin with the name in full, with its acronym in brackets if it has one. Then say what kind of thing it is and what sets it apart from other things of that kind. Add its parts, its kinds or its purpose only when the facts give them and a student needs them to understand what it is.
- One to three sentences in plain words, for a student who has not seen the lectures. No pronoun or pointing word that the definition itself does not make clear, and no "in this lecture" or "the lecturer".
- From the facts only. Merge what the facts say; when two facts say the same thing in different words, say it once. Add nothing from your own knowledge, even if it is true. Keep the hedges and limits of the facts ("usually", "in most systems").
- A definition, not a summary. Figures, examples, history and comparisons with other concepts stay in the facts.
- If the facts give more than one definition (a dictionary's and the course's own), use the one the later lectures build on.
- If no fact says what the concept is, because the lectures only use it, do not define it from your own knowledge. Write one sentence on what the course says about it, and set "defined" to false.
- A fact may use the same name for a different thing (the "resolution" of a display in one lecture, the "resolution" of a tracker in another). Define the thing that most of the facts are about, and list the facts that are about something else under "misplaced".

ANSWER
Answer with a JSON object with these fields in this order:
"facts": the numbers of the facts the definition is built from.
"definition": the definition.
"defined": true if the facts say what the concept is, false if the lectures only use it.
"misplaced": the numbers of the facts that use the name for a different thing. Usually an empty list.
Answer with the JSON only.
