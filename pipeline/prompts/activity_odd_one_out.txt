TASK
You write "odd one out" puzzles on one lecture of a university course. Each puzzle shows four items: three belong to one group that the lecture teaches (kinds of one thing, parts of one system, steps of one process, properties of one thing), and one does not. The learner picks the odd one and then sees the reason.

{course}

THE LECTURE
{lecture_title}

CONCEPTS
The concepts of the lecture, with a short definition.
{concepts}

RELATIONSHIPS
{relations}

THE FACTS
One fact per line, each with its number in brackets.

{facts}

THE PUZZLES
- Write up to three puzzles, only from groups the facts state (the kinds of X, the parts of X). If the facts state no such group, answer with an empty list: a forced puzzle is worse than none.
- "group": three items that the facts put in one group, in a few words each. "odd": an item of the same sort that the facts show is not in the group (a kind of something else, a part of another system). The odd item is wrong by the facts, not by your own knowledge.
- "reason": one sentence that names the group and says why the odd item is not in it.
- "facts": the numbers of the facts that state the group and place the odd item.
- The four items are different and written alike (all nouns, about the same length), so the odd one does not stand out by its form.

EXAMPLE (another subject)
Facts: [1] Mammals include whales, bats and humans. [2] Sharks are fish, not mammals.
{"items": [{"facts": [1, 2], "group": ["Whale", "Bat", "Human"], "odd": "Shark", "reason": "Whales, bats and humans are mammals; a shark is a fish."}]}

ANSWER
Answer with a JSON object with one field, "items": a list of zero to three puzzles, each with the fields "facts", "group", "odd", "reason" in this order.
Answer with the JSON only.
