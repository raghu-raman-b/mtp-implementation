TASK
You write "odd one out" puzzles on one lecture of a university course. Each puzzle shows four items: three belong to one group that the lecture teaches (kinds of one thing, parts of one system, steps of one process, properties of one thing), and one does not. The learner sees only the four items, with no hint of what the group is, picks the odd one and then sees the reason. So the three members must bring their group to mind by themselves, and the odd item must be a near miss that only knowledge of the lecture rules out.

THE LECTURE
{lecture_title}

CONCEPTS
The concepts of the lecture, with a short definition.
{concepts}

RELATIONSHIPS
{relations}

THE FACTS
One fact per line, each with its number in brackets.

{facts}

THE PUZZLES
- Write up to three puzzles, each from a different group, and only from groups the facts state. If the facts state no such group, answer with an empty list: a forced puzzle is worse than none.
- "group": three members of one group, in a few words each. A fact names the group as a group of the subject: the kinds of X, the parts of X, the steps of X, the properties of X ("X has three ...", "the kinds of X are ...", "X consists of ..."). Not things that only happen to be mentioned together: the features of one product, the examples in one illustration, the places or people in one story. Being absent from such a list does not put an item outside a group.
- "odd": an item of the same sort and level as the three (a kind among kinds, a part among parts, a device among devices), for which the facts say what it is instead: a kind of something else, a part of another system, a step of another process. A learner who half remembers the lecture could take it for a member. The odd item is wrong by the facts, not by your own knowledge. It is never:
  - the name of the group itself, or the whole that the three are parts of ("engine" beside three parts of an engine);
  - a thing of another sort (a unit of measurement beside three measuring instruments);
  - an item the facts simply do not mention with the group;
  - an item from so far away that anyone could spot it without the lecture.
- One answer only. Check that no other item can be singled out by another reasonable grouping (three are hardware and one is software, but not the item you chose), and that no fact lets a learner argue the odd item into the group. If another choice can be defended, drop the puzzle. Do not use the same four items in two puzzles with a different odd item.
- Written alike: the four items are the same kind of phrase, about the same length, each beginning with a capital letter. The odd item must not stand out by its form: when the three members share a word that the odd item lacks ("... acid" three times and one "enzyme"), the wording gives the answer away, so choose an odd item that carries the word too, or another group.
- "reason": one sentence that names the group and says what the odd item is instead, as a statement about the subject: not "the facts state", "is listed as", "is described as" or "in the lecture".
- "facts": the numbers of the facts that state the group and place the odd item.

EXAMPLE (another subject)
Facts: [1] Mammals include whales, bats and humans. [2] Sharks are fish, not mammals.

ANSWER
Answer with a JSON object with one field, "items": a list of zero to three puzzles, each with the fields "facts", "group", "odd", "reason" in this order.
Answer with the JSON only.
