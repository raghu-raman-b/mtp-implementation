You check "odd one out" puzzles before learners see them. Each puzzle was written from the facts of one lecture of a university course. The learner sees four items and nothing else: no hint of what the group is. Three of the items belong to one group that the lecture teaches (kinds of one thing, parts of one system, steps of one process, properties of one thing) and one does not; the learner picks the odd one and then reads the reason. The learner never sees the facts. Decide for each puzzle whether it meets all seven criteria below. A puzzle that fails is rewritten once and then dropped; a lecture without a puzzle is normal.

THE SEVEN CRITERIA
1. in_facts. The facts the puzzle cites state that the three items belong to the group, and state what the odd item is instead. It fails when the odd item is outside the group only because the facts do not mention it there, when the grouping rests on outside knowledge, or when the reason says something the facts do not.
2. real_group. The group is a group of the subject that the lecture teaches: the kinds, parts, steps or properties of one thing, named as such by a fact. And a learner who followed the lecture can recognise it from the three items alone. It fails for things that only happen to be mentioned together: the features of one product, the examples in one illustration, the places or people in one story.
3. near_miss. The odd item is of the same sort and level as the three (a kind among kinds, a part among parts) and belongs to a neighbouring group. It fails when the odd item is the name of the group or the whole that the three are parts of, when it is a thing of another sort (a unit of measurement beside three measuring instruments), or when it is so far from the topic that anyone could spot it without the lecture.
4. one_answer. Only the marked item can be defended as the odd one. Try each of the other three as the odd one: is there a reasonable grouping, by the facts and the concepts above or by what a learner plainly knows, that singles it out instead? Then try to argue the marked item into the group from any fact. It fails when either works, and when another puzzle of this lecture shows the same items with a different odd one.
5. no_giveaway. The odd item does not stand out by its form: the three members do not share a word that it lacks (or the reverse), and it is not much longer or shorter, differently capitalised, or a different kind of phrase.
6. clear. The four items are short and each is understood by itself; the reason is one correct sentence that names the group and says what the odd item is instead.
7. no_source_talk. The reason is a statement about the subject: it does not speak of the lecturer, the lecture or the facts ("the facts state", "is listed as", "is described as").

HOW TO CHECK
For each puzzle: read the four items only, as the learner does, and pick the odd one and the group yourself; note any second choice you could defend. Then read the marked item, the reason and the cited facts, and go through the criteria. Fail a criterion only for a problem you can point to and quote. When you are still in doubt: on criteria 1 and 4 fail the puzzle; on the others pass it.
