TASK
You check "put in order" exercises before learners see them. Each exercise was written from the facts of one lecture of a university course. The learner sees its title and, under it, the steps shuffled, and puts them back in order; the learner never sees the facts. An exercise is worth showing only when the order is something the lecture teaches and when exactly one order is right. Decide for each exercise whether it meets all seven criteria below. An exercise that fails is rewritten once and then dropped; a lecture without an exercise is normal.

THE SEVEN CRITERIA
1. in_facts. Every step is stated by the facts the exercise cites, and so is the place of every step in the order. It fails when a step is only implied or comes from outside the facts, or when the order rests on general knowledge of the subject.
2. real_sequence. What is ordered is a sequence: the steps of a procedure, the stages of a pipeline, the path something travels, events in time, phases that follow one another, and the cited facts say what comes after what ("first", "then", "after", "before", "in the order", numbered stages, dates). It fails for the kinds, parts, factors or properties of a thing; for a problem with its cause, its solution and what the solution needs or brings; for things done in a project when no fact says which came after which; and when the only ground for the order is the order in which a fact lists things that could be done in any order.
3. one_order. Exactly one order is right. Take each pair of neighbouring steps and ask whether the two could be swapped without contradicting a fact: if so, it fails. It also fails when two steps describe the same moment or one event is split in two, and when a scale, a range or a stack of layers is ordered and the title does not say at which end to start.
4. no_giveaway. Nothing in the wording sorts the steps without understanding: no "first", "then", "next", "finally", "initial", "final", no step numbers, dates or years, no "the previous step", and no title that reveals the order.
5. worth_doing. The order has to be learned from the lecture. It fails when someone who never followed the lecture could order the steps from the wording or from common sense ("the task is started", "the task is executed", "the task is completed"; "input is given", "it is processed", "output is produced").
6. clear. The title is a short instruction that names in full what is ordered and stands alone; the steps are short, grammatical, alike in form, and each can be understood by itself.
7. no_source_talk. Neither the title nor a step speaks of the lecturer, the lecture, the segment, the video or the facts.

HOW TO CHECK
For each exercise: read the cited facts and mark the words in them that state an order. Then, without looking at the order given, order the steps yourself from those facts; note every pair you could not place from the facts. Then compare with the order given and go through the criteria. Fail a criterion only for a problem you can point to and quote. When you are still in doubt: on criteria 1 to 3 fail the exercise; on criteria 4 to 7 pass it.
