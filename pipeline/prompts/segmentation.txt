You are splitting the transcript of one lecture from a university course into segments that learners can watch on their own.

Course: {course}

The transcript is below, one sentence per line, each with its number in brackets.

WHAT A SEGMENT IS
- A segment is the stretch of the lecture in which the lecturer achieves one sub-goal of the lecture. A sub-goal is a step toward the goal of the whole lecture: something the learner can do after that stretch, such as explain a concept, describe how something works, compare two approaches, or solve a type of problem.
- Find the sub-goals at the level of the lecture's sections, the parts the lecturer would give a heading of their own. A single example, a remark or an aside is not a sub-goal. Items of a list taught under one heading (three challenges, four stages) together make one sub-goal, unless the lecturer develops an item at length as a part of its own.

WHAT IS NOT A SUB-GOAL
- The greeting, the course name, the lecturer introducing themselves, a recap of the previous lecture and an outline of this lecture are opening remarks. The closing summary, a preview of the next lecture and the goodbye are closing remarks. They are not about achieving a sub-goal of this lecture.
- Never make opening or closing remarks a segment of their own: the opening remarks belong to the first segment and the closing remarks to the last segment.

WHERE TO CUT
- Cut where a sub-goal has been achieved and closed: the lecturer has finished it, often with a concluding remark ("So, that is how tracking works."), and moves on to the next one.
- A bridge belongs to the segment that follows it. A bridge is a sentence that leads into the next part: it announces, motivates or asks about what comes next ("Now, let us see how we can measure presence.", "So, what is the problem with this approach?", "Next, we will talk about displays."). The new segment starts at the bridge, not after it. A concluding remark stays with the part it concludes.
- Never cut inside an explanation, a derivation, a worked example, a list being explained, or between a question and its answer.

EACH SEGMENT MUST STAND ALONE
- A learner must be able to watch any segment by itself, without having seen the segment before it.
- So a segment must not open by pointing back to the previous segment: no "this", "that", "these", "such", "the above", "as we just saw", "the same", "it" or "they" whose meaning is only given in the previous segment. A segment may build on what earlier parts taught when it names it ("Head-mounted displays, which we discussed earlier, ...").
- If the sentence where you want to start a segment depends on the sentences just before it, the cut is in the wrong place: move it back to where that thought begins, or do not cut there.

HOW TO WORK
1. Read the whole transcript. Decide the goal of the lecture and list its sub-goals in the order they are taught.
2. For each sub-goal, find the sentence where it is closed and the sentence where the next one begins, including its bridge.
3. Check every segment: it achieves one sub-goal; it ends where that sub-goal is closed; it starts with its own bridge; it can be understood alone; no segment is only opening or closing remarks.

ANSWER
Answer with a JSON list with one object per segment, in order. "start" is the number of the segment's first sentence; the first segment starts at 1. "subgoal" says in one short sentence what the learner can do after the segment, starting with a verb ("Explain why ...", "Describe how ...").
Answer with the JSON only. Example of the format:
[{"start": 1, "subgoal": "Explain what extended reality means and how VR, AR and MR relate to it."}, {"start": 38, "subgoal": "Describe how a head-mounted display creates a sense of presence."}]
