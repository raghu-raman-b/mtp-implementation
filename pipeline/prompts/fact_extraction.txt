You extract atomic facts from one segment of a university lecture transcript.
A fact is a single teachable proposition about the course subject that the
lecturer establishes in this segment.

Requirements:
1. Established. The lecturer asserts it as true. Not a hypothesis, a
   question, an opinion or view, or something the lecturer only asks about.
2. Grounded. Cite the smallest set of segment sentence numbers that state
   the fact.
3. Atomic. One proposition per fact: it can be tested on its own and cannot
   be split into smaller facts. Keep the conditions, qualifiers, quantities
   and scope that make it true. Do not split one definition or one list of
   members across several facts.
4. Self-contained. A reader who has not seen the lecture must understand it.
   Replace pronouns and pointing words (this, it, here, that method, the
   slide) with the specific term. Do not write "the lecturer says".
5. Canonical. Write every term in full words, never as an acronym or
   abbreviation, even where the lecturer uses one: "extended reality", not
   "XR" or "extended reality (XR)".
6. On-subject only. The fact is about the segment goal and the course
   subject. Skip greetings, logistics, anecdotes, jokes, rhetorical
   questions, and promises of later coverage ("we will see this next week").
7. Faithful. Add nothing the lecturer did not say, even if it is true. Keep
   hedges ("usually", "in most cases"). If the lecturer corrects themselves,
   keep only the corrected version.
8. No repeats. If a point is made twice, output one fact citing all its
   sentences.
9. If the segment establishes nothing, return an empty list.

Where it fits, write each fact as one declarative sentence in the order
subject, relation, predicate: what the fact is about, then the verb that
relates it ("is", "covers", "consists of", "causes"), then the rest.

Write the evidence before the text. Output JSON only:
{"facts": [{"evidence": [int, ...], "text": "..."}]}

### Example
COURSE TITLE: Virtual Reality
THE COURSE
Use this to understand what the course covers as a whole: its goals and the order of its topics.
Outline: The course explains the foundations of virtual and augmented reality systems: their concepts, hardware and design.
Syllabus (the planned topics in order; a lecture may cover part of a topic or several topics):
- Introduction: Immersive technologies; History
- Hardware: Displays; Tracking
LECTURE: Introduction to immersive technologies
SEGMENT GOAL: Distinguish XR and AR from VR
SEGMENT:
[1] Okay, so now there is this other term you will keep hearing, XR, extended reality.
[2] It is basically an umbrella term.
[3] It covers VR, AR, MR, and honestly whatever comes next in this space.
[4] I remember when I first tried a headset in 2014, I felt sick in five minutes, anyway.
[5] So AR, augmented reality, here you still see the real world and the virtual content is overlaid on top of it.
[6] We will do the tracking part of this in week five.
OUTPUT:
{"facts": [
{"evidence": [1, 2, 3],
"text": "Extended reality is an umbrella term that covers virtual reality, augmented reality, mixed reality and future technologies of the same kind."},
{"evidence": [5],
"text": "Augmented reality lets the user still see the real world, with virtual content overlaid on top of it."}
]}
