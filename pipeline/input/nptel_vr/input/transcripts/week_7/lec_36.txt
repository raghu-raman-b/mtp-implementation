So, earlier we have learned what contextual inquiry is, that is to observe users in their natural work setting, observe targeted groups of users or specified groups of users in their natural work setting to understand how they behave and what they do. Now let us see what the different ways contextual inquiry can be performed are. So there are broadly two ways for performing contextual inquiry. One is the active mode. In this mode, the observer or the person who is performing the contextual inquiry is physically present in the environment where the users are doing their tasks.
There is another passive mode; here user activities are recorded or videotaped and So, there is no observer as such, but the activities are recorded and then observed later by the observer. If there are some issues, those can be clarified through interaction with the users. One important thing here is that during the inquiry, the observer should act as an apprentice, and treat the users as masters. So, this is called the master apprentice model. The purpose is to learn the user behavior, not to teach users how to behave.
So, it is not easy. We often get this behavioral trait that whenever somebody is doing something, we often get the urge to tell the user, okay, do not do it in this way, do it in that way, that will be better for you. That type of advisory is not desirable. In fact, these are prohibited when you are trying to perform a genuine contextual inquiry. So, ideally you should always assume that you are the learner and the users are the teachers. So, they are teaching you how they behave and how typically users would be behaving in a given context and you will learn that behavior from them rather than trying to teach them how to behave.
So, that is about different categories. Now, let us talk about the stages of the contextual inquiry. So there are five stages. These stages are basically a systematic way of carrying out the inquiry process. First is plan, then initiate, execute, close, and reflect. So the inquiry starts with the plan stage. It goes to the initiate stage, then the actual inquiry takes place in the execute stage. Then it is closed, and then finally reflect means analyzing the data. So in the plan stage, what are the things to do typically?
Plan, as the name suggests, means planning the activities. So, we first start with identification of the goals of observation, what we are trying to observe. That needs to be first identified. Otherwise, if we directly reach there, reach into the workplace, and try to start observing, then we will be confused about what to actually observe. So, first you plan for what exactly we are trying to observe. The second part is gathering knowledge about the task domain. So, what kind of task domain, how the domain is organized, all these things need to be known.
Also, plan for arrangements for recording observations if required, or noting down observations, how to do that; all these things need to be planned. An essential part of the planning is the identification of the users, and the date, time, and place of the observation. Certainly this is one of the most essential components of the plan stage and it always helps to have a script of the procedure and rehearse the script so that on the day of observation it goes as per plan. So scripting of the procedure always helps.
In the initiate stage, that is the second stage, We should contact the authorities for permissions, all necessary permissions, because we will be observing the employees in their work setting. And open communication with the end users to remove their anxieties. So, end users often believe that these observations may lead to something bad for them. So, all necessary permissions from relevant authorities must be obtained beforehand.
The execute test stage is the one where we actually perform the observation. So, performing observations and recording data takes place in the execute stage.
