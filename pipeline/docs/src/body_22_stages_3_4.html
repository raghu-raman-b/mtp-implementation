3a–3c

The concept registry and the synonym merge

codeembedderQwen 3.8 27B

Stage 2c names concepts segment by segment, so “headset” in lecture 1 and “head-mounted display” in lecture 2 are two entries. Stage 3 joins them into the course's concepts, in four steps, and rebuilds the relationships over the joined concepts.

  1. Exact merge (3a, code). Same normalised name: case, hyphens, slashes, punctuation and a plural ending ignored (“lens” = “lenses”, “head set” = “headsets”). A name that is an acronym is never merged by name.
  2. Candidates (3a, embedder). Each entry is embedded as <name>; also called: <other names>. <up to 5 defining facts> with Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B. A pair is a candidate at cosine ≥ 0.70 or when it shares a name or alias. Clusters are the connected candidates, at most 12 entries; larger components are split at their weakest links, and the cut pairs are listed (they can never merge).
  3. Same or different (3b, model). One call per cluster: the entries with their names, lectures and up to 8 facts (defining facts first, marked *). The answer groups them, gives a reason per group and the group's name.
  4. Merge (3c, code). One concept per group, C0001… in course order; losing names become aliases. Relationships are rebuilt from the 2c triples through segment_concepts.json: a triple that now joins a concept to itself is dropped, identical triples are joined. Every decision is a line in decisions.jsonl.

Defined and connecting concepts, and the glossary rule

Most terms a course names it never explains: “discount rate” in a list of policy tools, “gigabyte” in a spec. The prompt says not to define such a concept from the model's own knowledge, but to write one sentence on what the facts show about it, and set defined to false. These 1,806 connecting concepts stay on the concept maps, flagged connecting; the glossary lists only the 1,316 defined ones (viewer rule since commit 194d12a). Since the decision of 2026-11-18 they have no card, their sentence is never sent to the page, and question answering gets their facts only (What fails where).

4c

The lecture graph

code

Lecture K is needed for lecture L when L uses a concept that K defines and K comes first. The link carries those concepts. A concept used everywhere should not make every lecture depend on lecture 1, so it weighs less:

Hub weighting

With N lectures and n lectures using a concept, free = max(3, 0.25·N). The concept weighs 1 when n ≤ free, else 1 − 0.9·(n − free)/(N − free), down to 0.1. A link is a prerequisite when its concepts weigh at least 1; lighter links are kept as weak. Direct prerequisites are those not implied through another lecture (transitive reduction); the rest are indirect. Defaults proposed, D10.

A lecture's concepts fall into four kinds: introduced (first defined here), met_again (defined earlier), defined_later, assumed (never defined). Lecture 30 has 23, 7, 0 and 15. Recall activities and “what do I need before lecture N” answers read this. A weakness found by the QA work: a link counts any single mention, so lists of prerequisites held odd items; the QA tool now ranks earlier concepts by how often the lecture uses them and lists single mentions as “in passing” (QA).
