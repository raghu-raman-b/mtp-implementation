2a

Facts: what each segment teaches

Qwen 3.8 27B

A fact is one complete point of subject knowledge that the lecturer teaches in the segment: something a student should know after it and could be asked about in an exam. Facts are the store everything else is built from, so each one must be worth looking up on its own and must cite every sentence that states it. One request per segment.

The prompt states the eight properties of a fact (significant, whole, established, grounded, self-contained, terms in full, on the subject, faithful), asks for subject–relation–predicate sentences, and gives two worked examples from other subjects with the reasons sentences were left out. Code maps the local numbers back to sentence IDs.

2b

Fact verification, and why it is switched off

built · off

Stage 2b was designed to judge each segment's facts against the eight properties with a second model (Sage-A1.5, a 35B mixture-of-experts, on port 8095), and have the extractor rewrite or remove the ones that fail. It is built and tested. In the course as built it runs with --no-judge: the 2a facts pass through unchanged, keeping their IDs, and each judgement reads “not judged”.

Why it is off: block 1's measurement

Block 1 measured the judge on the 62 facts of lectures 1, 4 and 16 against a hand-made gold set (55 pass, 7 fail), 34 facts made wrong on purpose, and three runs with different seeds (analysis/fact_judge/report.md):

The reviewer's finding (Fable, gate review 1)

“2b's net effect on the 62 facts is negative … Of 8 facts acted on, 7 are gold-pass. … 0 of 7 gold failures properly fixed, 6 never touched.” A sentence that tracking is also called sensing was removed on a verdict that flips; the lecturer's “may not be required” stayed “is not required”. The judge checks that words are present, not how strong or wide a statement is: the right reason for a Faithful failure appeared in 2 of 30 verdicts.

On 2026-11-13 the MVP skipped the judge (--no-judge), at the user's decision after that review. Block 1 continues separately: the judge and rewrite prompts were revised with the reviewer's exact text (changes 1–7), the gold gained 13 subtle seeded errors and labels for the 8 rewrites (changes 8, 9). The rerun is pending. The cost of leaving it off is visible: facts like 1/S0/F0 (“The full forms of the abbreviations are …”) stay in the store.

2c

Concepts and relationships per segment

Qwen 3.8 27B

One conversation per segment. Turn 1 lists the concepts the segment's facts name: terms of the subject a textbook glossary would list, each with its name, aliases, the facts that name it and whether the facts define it. Code checks the list. Turn 2 gets the checked names back and lists the relationships the facts state, as triples whose subject, label and object read as one sentence.

Changes and why

  • Names are full terms; acronyms are only aliases (D8, 2026-11-13): “virtual reality headset”, not “VR headset”. Without it, an acronym name joins unrelated concepts in stage 3 (v1's “Error rates”).
  • Every answer is checked and retried with the problems shown (D12), and the best attempt is kept when none passes. The appears test was fixed for apostrophes and “X (Y) Z” forms: on the trial's answers false alarms fell from 8 to 0 (alias_not_written) and from 9 to 4 (name_not_in_facts).
  • Labels are free text (the user's choice of 2026-11-12, D6), so the same relation can appear under several wordings: stage 3c finds 827 concept pairs with more than one label.
  • Relationships only within a segment (D4): bounded prompts, at the cost of links between segments.
