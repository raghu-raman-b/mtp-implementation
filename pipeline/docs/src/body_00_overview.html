Pipeline v3 · documentation · as of 2026-11-17

From lecture video to a course that answers back

Pipeline v3 reads a course's subtitles and builds a knowledge layer: the facts each lecture teaches, the concepts they name, how those concepts relate, what each lecture is for and which lectures need which. On top of it sit activities checked by a judge, and a question box that answers only from the course and cites the lecture and the minute. Every claim traces back to a sentence of a lecture.

The pipeline in five minutes

A course goes in as subtitle files. It comes out as a folder, knowledge/, that a program can query, plus activities built from it. The heavy model work happens once, on a GPU server. Answering a learner's question later needs only a small model and the folder.

The idea in one paragraph

Do the expensive, careful work once: cut each lecture where its goals change, extract what it teaches as facts with evidence, name the concepts, join their names across lectures, write one definition per concept, and work out which lectures depend on which. Store it all with IDs that lead back to sentences and times. Then every later use (an answer, a quiz, a concept map, a list of prerequisites) is a small, checkable step over that store, and every claim it makes can be shown at the moment of the video where it was said.
