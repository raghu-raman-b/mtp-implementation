[1/S0/F0] The full forms of the abbreviations are extended reality (XR), virtual reality (VR), augmented reality (AR), and mixed reality (MR), respectively.
[1/S0/F1] Extended reality (XR), virtual reality (VR), augmented reality (AR), and mixed reality (MR) can refer to different things to different people: for some, magical movie scenes; for some, three-dimensional (3D) or four-dimensional (4D) movie-theater documentaries about extinct animals; and, for virtual reality (VR), the most common perception is that a user wears a headset that blocks vision and is transported to a virtual world.
[1/S1/F0] Extended Reality (XR) is an umbrella term, not a specific technology, that covers all present or future immersive technologies, such as augmented reality (AR), virtual reality (VR), and mixed reality (MR).
[1/S1/F1] The X in Extended Reality (XR) can be read as a variable, not only as part of 'extended', and its values such as A, V, and M denote augmented reality (AR), virtual reality (VR), and mixed reality (MR), while its many possible values form present or future immersive technologies.
[1/S1/F2] An immersive experience is a perceived experience through sensory organs that appears real: when a person experiences an environment through sensory organs and fails to distinguish that environment from the person's real environment, perceiving it as the real environment, the technology producing that experience is an immersive technology.
[1/S2/F0] The reality-virtuality continuum is a scale whose extremes are a completely real environment with nothing virtual and a completely virtual environment with nothing real.
[1/S2/F1] Augmented reality on the reality-virtuality continuum is the intermediate state in which the real environment is augmented with virtual content.
[1/S2/F2] Augmented virtuality on the reality-virtuality continuum is the intermediate state, opposite augmented reality, in which the virtual environment is augmented with real-world content.
[1/S2/F3] Mixed reality, in a broad sense, is the entire space between the real environment extreme and the virtual environment extreme of the reality-virtuality continuum, containing different degrees of augmentation of the real environment with virtual content and of the virtual environment with real content.
[1/S3/F0] A 2022 forecast estimated the share of content related to extended reality technologies in different application domains as video games 34 percent, healthcare 15 percent, engineering 14 percent, live events 12 percent, video entertainment 9 percent, real estate 7 percent, retail 5 percent, and military 4 percent, and these figures may now be larger.
[1/S3/F1] A market study reports that the extended reality market size was about 20.5 billion US dollars in 2021 and is expected to be about 860 billion US dollars by 2030, an exponential increase.
[1/S3/F2] The hardware market in the extended reality market is about 60 percent of the market size, and the software market is about 40 percent.
[1/S3/F3] In India, the extended reality industry was estimated at about 1.83 billion US dollars in 2020 and is expected to be about 14 billion US dollars in 2027.
[1/S3/F4] The term metaverse has heavily stimulated and spurred growth in the extended reality field.
[1/S3/F5] The metaverse is not merely an application of virtual reality, mixed reality, or augmented reality technology.
[1/S3/F6] One broad point of view about the metaverse is that humans are going to reimagine the way they interact with the digital world.
[1/S3/F7] In the metaverse, people act as they do in the real world in an all-around three-dimensional environment and use natural interaction with digital content, unlike current mostly flat two-dimensional screen interactions with occasional three-dimensional interaction.
[1/S3/F8] The metaverse is not yet fully realized because current technology is not advanced enough and has technological bottlenecks, but companies are investing heavily to make it a reality, with growth potential in both hardware and software.
[1/S3/F9] The growth of extended reality creates a need for more knowledgeable and skilled manpower who can develop and deploy systems based on the technology.
[1/S3/F10] The extended reality field is one of the major emerging areas identified by many national governments, along with artificial intelligence (AI), large language models, generative artificial intelligence, drones, and nanomaterials.
[1/S5/F0] The seminal reference for the reality-virtuality continuum is the 1994 paper 'A Taxonomy of Mixed Reality Visual Displays' by Paul Milgram and Fumio Kishino, one of the earliest papers in the field of mixed reality visual displays, which significantly influenced the development of that field.
