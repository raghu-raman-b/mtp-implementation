  Q How does the main purpose of tracking differ between virtual reality and augmented reality?  [16/S0/F2, 16/S0/F3; virtual reality, augmented reality, tracking, pose]
    A In virtual reality, tracking is primarily about translating the user's physical motion or input into virtual motion in the virtual world. In augmented reality, tracking is primarily about registering virtual content in three-dimensional space with respect to an anchor in the real world. Augmented reality therefore also requires tracking the user's pose, position, and orientation, as well as moving real-world objects in the field of view.
  Q Why does outside-in tracking limit the user to the area covered by its sensors, while inside-out tracking does not?  [16/S2/F2, 16/S2/F4, 16/S2/F5; outside-in tracking, inside-out tracking, external sensor, sensor range]
    A Outside-in tracking uses external sensors in the real world, such as cameras or markers, and it works only when the user is within the sensor range of those external sensors. Inside-out tracking locates the required cameras or other sensors within the experience device, such as a head-mounted display, or attached to the user. Because those sensors move with the user, there is no fixed external sensor area that restricts the tracked experience.
  Q How do calibration and drift correction each reduce error in the tracking process?  [16/S3/F1, 16/S3/F2, 16/S3/F5; calibration, drift correction, sensor error, estimation error]
    A Calibration reduces sensor error by transforming the output of imperfect sensors so that it behaves as closely as possible to the natural or real value. Drift correction reduces estimation error by compensating for the difference between the estimated and actual position and orientation of the user, sense organs, body parts, or environment objects. These are separate steps in the tracking process.
  Q Why is environment tracking especially important in augmented reality when a physical object moves in the real-world view?  [16/S1/F7, 16/S1/F8, 16/S1/F9; environment tracking, augmented reality, physical object, digital content]
    A In augmented reality, a moved physical object must be tracked in the real-world view so that digital content can be registered appropriately in three-dimensional space. This makes environment tracking more important in augmented reality than in virtual reality, although virtual reality can also use it. For example, in virtual reality it can alert the user to physical objects not represented in the virtual world, such as a wall, to reduce harm.
