[24/17] Let us quickly go through one interesting example.
[24/18] All of you might have used some word processing system such as MS Word.
[24/19] Now, if you have noticed, there are different ways to perform the same task in any word processing system.
[24/20] Let us take the example of MS Word.
[24/21] It is equally applicable for other processing applications as well.
[24/22] Suppose you have created a file.
[24/23] Now you want to save it.
[24/24] How can you save it?
[24/25] There are multiple options provided.
[24/26] One option is the combination of hotkeys.
[24/27] That is, you press the CTRL and S keys together on the keyboard to save the file.
[24/28] That is a hotkey combination.
[24/29] There is another way of saving it.
[24/30] That is, you can use the file menu option.
[24/31] Under this, you can use the save option.
[24/32] Then a dialog appears.
[24/33] You provide the details and you can save it.
[24/34] So, through the menu option also you can save.
[24/35] Now, have you ever wondered why these multiple options are given when any one option is good enough to do the job?
[24/36] The answer lies in what we have just discussed, that is, these options are given to cater to different groups of users.
[24/37] Typically, when we are talking about novice users, they are basically the users who are using it for the first time.
[24/38] For novice users, remembering hotkey combinations is generally considered difficult.
[24/39] So, they have to recall the knowledge.
[24/40] Because they are using it for the first time, they are unlikely to have created that knowledge itself.
[24/41] So, there is no question of recalling that this hotkey combination will do the job.
[24/42] So, this menu option is given through which they can save it.
[24/43] Of course, that is, It takes a longer time as compared to the hotkey combination, but for novice users, it is suitable.
[24/44] For intermittent or expert users who have used the system for a longer duration or multiple times, the hotkey combination may provide a better alternative as compared to the menu option.
[24/45] Particularly for expert users, for them the hotkey combination is always preferable as compared to the menu option.
[24/46] Now, one thing to note here is that when we are talking about expert users, they are not really technology experts, rather they are experts in using the system.
[24/47] So, they might have used the system for quite some time, already understood all aspects of the system, and they can use it very efficiently.
[24/48] So the three categories—novice, intermittent, and expert—are one way of classifying.
[24/49] Just to recap, novice means first-time usage, intermittent means occasional usage, and expert means regular usage over a long period of time.
[24/50] Of course, this is only one way of categorization, and there are more fine categorizations possible.
[24/51] Better categorizations can be done based on empirical studies of the users, but this broad categorization serves important purposes when we are starting our design process.
