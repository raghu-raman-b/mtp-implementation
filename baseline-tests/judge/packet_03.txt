The course describes three types of fidelity in VR:

* **Representational fidelity:** This ranges from extremely low, where worlds are purely abstract and non-objective, to the highest level, which is indicated by photorealistic immersive film content like 360-degree video (lecture 9, 14:21).
* **Interaction fidelity:** This is the degree to which physical actions for a virtual task correspond to the actions for an equivalent real-world task. It ranges from very low fidelity (such as sitting while moving forward via button press) to high fidelity involving intense realistic physical actions (lecture 9, 15:12).
* **Experiential fidelity:** Low experiential fidelity occurs when a virtual world offers endless possibilities that do not happen in the real world, while higher experiential fidelity results from placing real-world constraints on virtual navigation, movement, and interaction (lecture 9, 16:12).

In VR design, the three levels of fidelity refer to how closely the virtual environment mimics reality:

1.  **Low Fidelity:** Focuses on basic functionality and layout using simple shapes and colors (e.g., wireframes or gray boxes).
2.  **Medium Fidelity:** Adds more detail, such as realistic textures, basic lighting, and improved animations.
3.  **High Fidelity:** Aims for photorealistic graphics, complex physics, and high-quality sensory details to create an immersive experience.
