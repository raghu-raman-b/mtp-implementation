## L04 [list, expect yes] What are the six qualities of immersion?
GOLD: Extensive (range of sensory modalities), matching (congruence between modalities, e.g. head movement and view), surrounding (panoramic cues, e.g. wide FOV), vivid (energy within a modality: resolution, lighting, frame rate), interactive (user can change the world and get responses) and plot-informing (a consistent scripted story) — from Slater and Wilbur's definition.
-- ours_v2 r1: In the definition originally proposed by Slater and Wilbur, immersion is the objective degree to which a virtual reality system projects stimuli onto the sensory receptors of users in the following six ways (lecture 8, 10:12):

* **Extensive**: The range of sensory modalities presented to the user (e.g., visual, auditory, touch, smell, and taste; lecture 8, 13:00).
* **Matching**: The congruence between sensory modalities, such as a match between a user's physical movement and movement in the virtual world (lecture 8, 14:01).
* **Surrounding**: The extent to which cues are panoramic, such as the visual field of view or the extent of auditory source perception (lecture 8, 15:34).
* **Vivid**: The amount of energy stimulated within a sensory modality, related to factors like resolution, lighting, frame rate, and audio bit rate (lecture 8, 16:46).
* **Interactive**: The user's capability to make changes to the virtual world and receive responses from virtual entities to their actions (lecture 8, 18:26).
* **Plot-informing**: The extent to which the experience is a scripted story that the user can understand or predict, including the consistent portrayal and dynamic unfolding of the world (lecture 8, 19:37).

## F01 [formula, expect yes] How is the System Usability Scale (SUS) score calculated?
GOLD: Ten statements rated 1-5. S = 2.5 x ( sum over odd statements of (r_i - 1) + sum over even statements of (5 - r_i) ). The score reads as a percentage; 70 or more is desirable.
-- ours_v2 r1: The System Usability Scale (SUS) score $S$ is calculated using the ratings $r_i$ from 10 numbered statements, where $i$ ranges from 1 to 10 (lecture 44, 9:46). 

The formula is:
$S = 2.5 \times (\sum_{i \in \{1,3,5,7,9\}}(r_i - 1) + \sum_{i \in \{2,4,6,8,10\}}(5 - r_i))$ (lecture 44, 9:46)

In this calculation:
* $r_i$ is the user's rating for statement $i$ on a 5-point agreement scale (lecture 44, 11:48).
* The even-numbered statements are negatively worded counterparts of the preceding odd-numbered statements (lecture 44, 9:30).
* The resulting score is interpreted directly as a percentage (lecture 44, 13:31).

## F03 [formula, expect yes] In the simple lighting model, how is the light intensity reaching the viewer computed?
GOLD: As the sum I = I_amb + I_diff + I_spec. Ambient = Ka x ambient intensity; diffuse = Kd x Is x (N . L) when N . L > 0 else 0; specular (Phong) = Ks x Is x (V . r)^Ns when V . r > 0 else 0, with r = 2(L . N)N - L; coefficients Ka, Kd, Ks in 0..1.
-- ours_v2 r1: In the simple lighting model, the light intensity reaching the viewer from a surface point is computed as the sum of three intensities: (lecture 56, 24:18).

These components are (lecture 56, 26:32):
* **Ambient light intensity ($I_{amb}$):** Computed by multiplying the ambient light intensity falling on a surface point by the ambient reflection coefficient ($K_a$).
* **Diffuse reflection intensity ($I_{diff}$):** Computed by multiplying the diffuse reflection coefficient for direct light ($K_d$), the source light intensity ($I_s$), and the dot product of the surface normal vector $N$ and the light source direction vector $L$ (provided $N \cdot L > 0$; otherwise, it is 0).
* **Specular reflection intensity ($I_{spec}$):** Computed using the Phong approximate model as the specular reflection coefficient for direct light ($K_s$) times the source light intensity ($I_s$) times the dot product of the viewer direction vector $V$ and the ideal specular reflection direction vector $r$ raised to the power $N_s$ (provided $V \cdot r > 0$; otherwise, it is 0).
