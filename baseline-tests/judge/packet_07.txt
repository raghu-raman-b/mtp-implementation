The Kalman filter fuses IMU and camera data by treating them as complementary sources of information in a prediction-correction cycle:

*   **Prediction (IMU):** The filter uses high-frequency IMU data (accelerometer and gyroscope) to predict the object's position and orientation at very fast intervals.
*   **Correction (Camera):** The filter then uses lower-frequency camera data (visual markers or features) to correct the accumulated drift and errors from the IMU.
*   **Weighting:** It uses a "Kalman Gain" to mathematically decide how much to trust each sensor based on their respective noise levels.

Vergence-accommodation conflict (VAC) is a primary cause of motion sickness and eye strain in VR systems. It occurs because the eyes perform two different actions that are normally synchronized in the real world but are mismatched in a virtual environment:

*   **Vergence:** The inward or outward rotation of the eyes to focus on an object's distance.
*   **Accommodation:** The changing of the lens shape to focus on the screen's fixed plane.

In VR, your eyes converge on a distant virtual object while the lenses force them to accommodate (focus) on a fixed screen nearby.
