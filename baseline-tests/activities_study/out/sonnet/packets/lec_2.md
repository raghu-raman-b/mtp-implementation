## Unit U03: open question

### Set X

X1. Question: Why are virtual reality headsets common in commercial immersive applications even though fully immersive systems provide stronger immersion?
   Reference answer: Virtual reality headsets are common in commercial immersive applications because most such applications are semi-immersive and cost-effective. A headset gives partial immersion by blocking some senses, such as sight, while ears and skin can still receive real-world signals. Fully immersive systems block all senses and are the costliest and commercially rare form.

X2. Question: Why is World of Warcraft an example of a non-immersive virtual reality system even when a player is psychologically absorbed in the game?
   Reference answer: World of Warcraft is non-immersive because it is a specific gaming application that runs on a regular computer or console with ordinary displays and input devices. The player controls characters or activities while retaining control of the physical environment, and the virtual environment does not directly interact with the user. Although such software can take the user's attention, the user's sensory organs remain open and the user remains physically attached to the real world.

X3. Question: How do virtual tourism and digital heritage preservation differ as immersive applications in travel and tourism?
   Reference answer: In travel and tourism, virtual tourism and digital heritage preservation serve different purposes. Virtual tourism lets users visit faraway places without physically going there. Digital heritage preservation targets heritage places that cannot be physically visited by large numbers of users or in all parts for preservation reasons; it lets users explore each part virtually, preserves the space digitally despite physical decay, and increases the space's lifespan and potential to be visited by more users.

   Reference answer: Semi-immersive systems are the most commonly used form of VR because they are the most cost-effective. In contrast, fully immersive systems are commercially rare because they are difficult to afford.

Y2. Question: How does the blocking of senses differ between non-immersive and semi-immersive virtual reality systems?
   Reference answer: In a non-immersive system, the user's eyes, ears, and other sensory organs remain open and the user remains physically attached to the real world. A semi-immersive system provides a partial experience by blocking some but not all senses, such as blocking the eyes while leaving the ears and skin exposed to real-world signals.

   Reference answer: Non-immersive systems provide a virtual experience where users keep control of their physical environment and their sensory organs remain open. Semi-immersive systems provide a partial immersive experience by blocking some sensory organs, such as the eyes, while other senses remain exposed to real-world signals.

### Set W

W1. Question: In the context of healthcare and heritage tourism, how do immersive technologies solve specific logistical or physical problems that traditional methods cannot?
   Reference answer: In healthcare, immersive technologies address the shortage of cadavers by providing simulations for medical training on body organs. In heritage tourism, these technologies allow for 'virtual tours,' which solve the problem of physical decay and limited access; they enable users to explore historical sites virtually, preserving the physical integrity of the site while still allowing for high-volume exploration.

W2. Question: Explain the distinction between 'non-immersive' and 'semi-immersive' systems. Why is a non-immersive system still considered a form of virtual reality?
   Reference answer: Non-immersive systems are considered VR because they provide psychological immersion; even though the user's physical senses remain open to the real world, their attention is so fully captured by the digital environment (like a console game) that they become oblivious to their surroundings. Semi-immersive systems, by contrast, provide partial physical immersion by blocking specific senses—most commonly vision through a VR headset—while other senses remain free to perceive the real world.

W3. Question: What are the primary trade-offs between fully immersive systems and semi-immersive systems regarding technology, cost, and commercial availability?
   Reference answer: Fully immersive systems provide the highest degree of 'virtual realism' by using complex hardware (helmets, gloves, body suits, and motion trackers) to block all senses, but they are the most expensive and thus have lower commercial availability. Semi-immersive systems are more cost-effective and are currently the most common commercial applications because they offer a balance of immersion (blocking vision) without the extreme cost of full-body sensory blockade.
