Let us first start our understanding with an understanding of the generic graphics system. When we say that we have a graphics system, what we mean, what are the major components, and also we will learn about a few basic terms in order to understand the process of rendering. So, this is what a generic graphics system may look like. We have a host computer, and we have the display. And in between, there are three major components: a display controller, video memory, and a video controller.
 
As you can see, the host computer or CPU sends display commands and may receive some interaction data. So, there is two-way communication between the display controller and the CPU. Now, the display controller sends some data to video memory for storage, and this data is utilized by the video controller to render an image on screen. There can be input devices using which the user is interacting with the scene. So, that is part of interactive graphics.
However, we will not pay much attention to the input part in this discussion to keep things simpler. Now, let us try to understand these three components: the display controller, video memory, and video controller. Let us start with the display controller. What is the idea? Image generation. So, when I say that I am rendering a scene on the screen, that is nothing but a 2D image displayed on a 2D screen.
Now, this image generation is a multi-stage process involving lots of computations. If all these computations are to be carried out by the CPU or the host computer, the CPU may get very little time for doing other computational tasks which are not related to rendering. Its time will be consumed by rendering-related computations. So, in such cases, the system cannot do much except rendering, which of course defeats the very purpose of having a computer.
To avoid such situations and increase the efficiency of the system, the process of rendering is usually carried out by a dedicated component of the system. That is popularly known as the graphics card. Typically, graphics cards come with their own processing unit called GPU or graphics processing unit. So, we have a dedicated component in a computer which deals with rendering-related computations that is typically called the graphics card, comprising a GPU or graphics processing unit. And that is what we are referring to as the display controller in a very generic way.
The CPU typically assigns any graphics rendering task to this separate graphics unit. which we are calling the display controller. So, if graphics-related tasks appear, the CPU simply passes them on to the display controller; the computation takes place in the GPU and necessary action is taken. So, the CPU does not get involved in the computation. So, that is the display controller, which is responsible for creating a representation of the image that can be rendered on the screen.
Then comes the second part. So, we talked about the display controller. Then we have video memory. Let us see what this is. Now, the display controller generates the image in a digital format: strings of zeros and ones.
Remember that a computer can only understand zeros and ones. And that is what the display controller outputs in the form of zeros and ones, a representation of the image. Now, the place where it is stored is called the video memory. which is a dedicated part of the overall memory hierarchy of the computer and is typically part of a separate graphics unit. Sometimes it is called the VRAM or video RAM in the graphics card.
So, modern graphics cards come with a memory component which is this video memory. So we have a display controller which generates the image representation in zeros and ones which gets stored in the video memory part, and then we have the video controller, the third component. Now, a display screen contains picture elements or pixels. Any graphic display screen generally comes with a grid of pixels. So, arranged in the form of a grid.
When these pixels are excited, now this term is a generic term, excited means activated. By some means, it can be electrical or electromechanical, etc. Those pixels start emitting some light with specific intensities, which gives us the sensation of a colored image on the screen. So, on the display screen we have a pixel grid that is activated to emit light which, when it reaches our eye, we perceive as a colored image on the screen. Now, the video controller, the unit that we are talking about, converts the digital image that is stored in the video memory to some analog voltages required to activate pixels.
or activate the pixel excitation mechanism. So, the video controller essentially controls the mechanism that activates the pixels, and this mechanism relies on the video memory content. That is a generic graphic system. Now, when we are talking about rendering, the rendering process can be thought of in two ways.
So, there are broadly two types of rendering. Accordingly, we can say there are broadly two types of graphic devices. Now, these types are determined based on the method used for excitation of pixels. How are the pixels excited? One is the vector scan device; the other one is the raster scan device.
 
What happens in a vector scan device? Such devices are also known as random scan, stroke writing, or calligraphic devices. So, here, any image is viewed as composed of continuous geometric primitives, such as lines or curves. So, when we are presented with an image, we can think of it as containing basic geometric shapes, such as lines and curves. Then the image is rendered by rendering these primitives.
So, essentially the mechanism to render the image is to activate pixels that represent these primitives, assuming that if we focus on the rendering of the primitives, then the whole image will be perceived. So, essentially the idea is to excite only selective pixels in the pixel grid that represent the primitives. The idea is illustrated here.
As you can see, suppose we have a line. Now, I want to display this line. Now, this line contains these pixels, but this is the whole pixel grid. So, each cell contains one pixel. However, to display or render this line, what a vector scan device would typically do is to excite only those pixels that define the line.
 
Other pixels will not be excited or activated. Clearly, it requires high precision, high precision to target the specific pixels and excite them, which makes the systems costly and typically unaffordable for mass production or mass consumption. So, these are used typically; these types of graphic devices are typically used in specialized settings where limited quantity is required to make graphic systems that are more affordable and correspondingly easily available commercially.
A separate type of rendering mechanism is used that is called raster scan devices. So, in a raster scan device, what happens is that the image is viewed as represented by the whole pixel grid, not only the selected pixels representing the primitives. All the pixels are considered for rendering. Not the selected pixels. So, correspondingly, we do not require that high precision to target and activate selected pixels.
So, this is achieved by considering pixels in sequence. Some sequence has to be maintained to activate all pixels with appropriate input so that all can emit light with appropriate intensity so that the overall perception comes. Now, typically, the sequence is from left to right, top to bottom. Now, this process of basically scanning or basically selecting all the pixels in sequence is called scanning or raster scanning. Let us see an example.
 
Similar to the previous one, we want to render this line. On this pixel grid. However, unlike in the previous case, here we will activate all pixels with this sequence: left to right and top to bottom. That means we will start activation from the top left pixel, then proceed to the next, next till the last pixel. Then we will come back to the first pixel in the next row, proceed next, next in that way to the last one.
Then come back to the first pixel in the next row, and so on, until we reach this bottom right pixel. So, this is the left-to-right, top-to-bottom scanning sequence. Here we are activating all pixels with different input so that they emit intensity with different, so that they emit light with different intensity, so that eventually we perceive that there is a line. Although all pixels are getting excited, we will be able to perceive the line because those pixels on the line will be excited in a different way as compared to the surrounding pixels.
So, here, since we are following a sequence and activating all pixels, we do not require precision. So, the corresponding rendering mechanism, rendering arrangement, is much, much cheaper as compared to the selective rendering required for vector scan devices. And all the commercial products that you get to use, such as your smartphone or television or any device with some sort of computer and display, typically comprise raster scan displays.
There is another term that we should know of, that is called the frame buffer. Earlier we talked of a video memory which stores the image in the form of zeros and ones. Now, when we are talking of the video memory for a raster scan system where we are storing the intensity values for all pixels in the grid, the more generally accepted or popular term is called the frame buffer. So, the frame buffer essentially is the video memory component of the raster scan system.
It contains one location corresponding to each pixel. So, for each pixel there is one memory location which stores the intensity in the form of a bit string of 0s and 1s. So, the size of the frame buffer is the size of the screen, or in other words, the size of the frame buffer is equal to the screen resolution. So, the frame buffer is the video memory for raster scan devices, and its size is exactly the same as the screen resolution.
Another important concept here is screen refreshing. Light emitted from pixel elements after excitation starts decaying over time. That is the physical property of the material which makes the pixels. Now, this decaying light leads to fading of the scene after some time. Although the image is not changing, the scene starts fading because the emitted light starts decaying over time.
Pixels in a scene may get excited at different points in time and hence may not fade in sync, which may lead to image distortion. So, when we are rendering on a raster scan device, we are following a left-to-right, top-to-bottom manner. So, excitation of the top-left pixel is done much ahead of the excitation of the bottom-right-most pixel. By the time the bottom-right-most pixel is excited, the top-left pixel may start decaying, and this may happen with other pixels as well.
So, correspondingly, the image that we finally get to see may not appear uniform and may appear distorted. That is another issue with this decaying. One is the scene starts fading; the second one is the scene may not be rendered without distortion. To avoid this, what is typically done is to keep on exciting the pixels periodically. This is called refreshing.
So, although the image is not changing, the scanning mechanism still happens periodically so that we get to see uniformity in the image rendered. The refresh rate is the number of times a scene is refreshed per second on the screen. It is typically represented in terms of hertz, and a good refresh rate for most devices is 60 hertz. However, as we noted earlier in one of our discussions on human factors, a higher refresh rate is always preferable for virtual reality because here the screen is much closer to the eyes as compared to typical computer usage where the screen and eye are reasonably at a distance. So, a higher refresh rate is always preferable for a VR experience, a better VR experience. Also, let us quickly understand what we are referring to when we talk of a color image. So, pixels are materials. When we are talking of color screens or color displays, we are talking of a specific thing: that each pixel contains more than one type of element.
And each type of element is capable of emitting light with different intensities than the other type of element. So, when excited, the three elements, typically three elements are there. although sometimes four can be used for different purposes. However, let us assume that there are three elements for each pixel. These three elements can emit light with three different intensities which typically represent the primary colors, that is, red, green, and blue.
Now, when we get to see these emissions when they come to our eye, Essentially, what happens is that this red, green, and blue get mixed up in our eye, and due to this mixing, we get to see a different color, which is a combination of different amounts of red, green, and blue. So, that is how we perceive color. However, more on it we will discuss in a later part of the lecture.
