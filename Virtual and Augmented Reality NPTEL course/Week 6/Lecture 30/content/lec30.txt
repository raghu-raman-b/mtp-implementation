One is psychophysics, the other one is psychoacoustics. So psychophysics is the scientific study of perceptual phenomena that are produced by physical stimuli. That is a broader definition.
Psychoacoustics is a more specific area of psychophysics where we can replace the term physical with the term acoustic. So in other words, Psychoacoustics is the scientific study of perceptual phenomena that are produced by acoustic stimuli or sound. Now, there is one famous law called Stevens' power law, which is one of the most well-known results from psychophysics. What does it tell?
It characterizes the relationship between the magnitude of physical stimulus and its perceived magnitude. So, actual magnitude of the physical stimulus, assuming that to be a wave, and its perceived magnitude, how these two are related. So, the relationship is represented in this form: P equals Cm to the power x, where M is the magnitude or intensity of the original stimulus. P is the perceived magnitude.
X relates actual magnitude to the perceived magnitude. And C is a constant that depends on units. So, this is called Stevens' power law to relate our perception to reality, to relate our perceived stimuli to actual stimuli. There is another important concept we should be aware of that is called just noticeable difference or JND.
This is another well-known result from psychophysics which says that the amount that the stimulus needs to be changed so that subjects would perceive it to have changed in at least 50% of trials. So, it refers to a kind of experimentation with some physical stimuli where  the just noticeable difference indicates the amount of change in the stimuli which the subjects can perceive in at least 50 percent of the trials. Now, there is another law to capture JND, a just noticeable difference that is called Weber's law, which is represented as delta M by M equals C.
Here, M is the magnitude or intensity of the stimulus. Delta M is the just noticeable difference, that is the change in magnitude which is noticeable, and C is a constant. So, in other words, we can determine delta M as M times C. So, M multiplied by the constant. Now, both of these laws, Weber's law and Stevens' Power Law, these are from psychophysics. However, as we have already seen, they are equally applicable to psychoacoustics or our auditory perception. So, if we replace physical stimuli with acoustic stimuli, then the laws will hold even if the nature of stimuli has changed. So, they are equally applicable to our auditory perception. The exponent for perceived loudness of a 3000 Hz pure tone is x equal to 0.67.
That is the Stevens' Power Law. This implies that if a sound increases to a much higher pressure level, we perceive it as only a bit louder as the exponent is less than 1. So, the perception P equal to C m to the power x. So, even if the physical stimuli changes to a higher degree, but perception-wise, that change will be much more moderate. That is what the power law tells. So then we can understand that to generate some effect, how much change we should try to make so that it is perceivable based on this type of psychophysical or psychoacoustic laws.
