Research has found that reducing the field of view can be an effective way to alleviate symptoms of cyber sickness. However, an excessively narrow field of view can lead to decreased immersion, which in turn leads to an increase in head movement to basically look for things in the environment, and that increase in head movement may be likely to exacerbate or increase CS.
Of course, it is a challenge to find the optimal FOV, but that is very crucial for creating a comfortable VR experience. Type of content is another important factor. What input do we assume for measuring cybersickness?

Now, these types of rating scales for measuring CS are quite common. They are easy to administer and help the researchers or practitioners to better understand the causes and identify potential interventions. Typically, these scales are administered before or after the experience. Sometimes they can be administered even during the experience as well.
So, before, during, and after all three are possible, but mostly before or after and occasionally during the experience. So, one common scale of measurement used for cyber sickness is SSQ which stands for Simulator Sickness Questionnaire. It is in fact one of the most extensively used questionnaires for the purpose. It contains 16 items or
questions divided into three subscales: oculomotor subscale, disorientation subscale, and nausea subscale. Participants are asked to rate the intensity of individual symptoms. belonging to these three subscales, oculomotor, disorientation, and nausea, on a scale of 0 to 3, where a higher score denotes more severe symptoms. Now, SSQ was not originally designed for VR; it was designed for some other purpose, but adopted or rather adapted to use in the VR context, but there are other questionnaires that are specifically designed for VR, say, the Virtual Reality Sickness Questionnaire or VRSQ, then
