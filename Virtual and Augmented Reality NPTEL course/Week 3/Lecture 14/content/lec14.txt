So, we have covered the input part, hand-based and non-hand-based, let us shift our focus to the processing part, that is the computer. Computers, in our context, are devices that process the data that are coming from the input sensors, and then based on that processing, it activates or actuates the output devices now. The processing elements or computers are selected according to the requirement. For example, depending on the application or user or input-output devices, availability of input-output devices or the level of immersion required, we can choose the corresponding processing element. Then we have haptic rendering systems, or systems that activate our touch sensation. Now, haptics are outputs exploring our touch perception, and it can be categorized in different ways.
The types of haptic outputs that we can use can be categorized in different ways. We can have passive versus active devices. We can have tactile versus proprioceptive devices. We can have self-grounded versus world-grounded devices. So, passive devices are static physical objects, whereas active devices are physical feedback controlled by the computer.
Tactile are touch sensations obtained through the skin. So, our skin gets activated, whereas proprioceptive forces are activated. Forces are those that activate our joints or muscles. Self-grounded means the devices are on the body, whereas world-grounded means they are attached to the real world; they cannot be moved around. So, when we talk of passive haptics, we are referring to real-world physical objects that match the shape of the virtual objects.
So, when we touch those real-world objects, we get a feel of interacting with the virtual objects. But those real-world physical objects are not controlled by the computer. Whereas in the case of active haptics, we have objects, physical objects that are controlled by computers which give us a sense of touch when we are dealing with virtual objects. So these are the more common forms of haptics used in VR experiences. When we talk about
tactile haptics, they are basically devices that provide a sense of touch through the skin. It can be of two types: vibrotactile stimulation and electrotactile stimulation. In the case of vibrotactile stimulation, we are using mechanical vibration of the skin, whereas in the case of electrotactile stimulation, we are using electrodes that pass current through the skin. As opposed to that, we can have proprioceptive force-based haptics. which provide a sense of limb movement and muscular resistance by providing corresponding signals to the muscles and joints.
Then we have self-grounded versus world-grounded devices. So, in self-grounded devices, as shown in this accompanying figure, we can see that these are worn or held by and move with the user. So, in the case of self-grounded, it is worn and movable; in the case of world-grounded, it is not movable.
