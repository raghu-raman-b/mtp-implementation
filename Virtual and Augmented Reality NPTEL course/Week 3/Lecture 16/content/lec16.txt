What do these steps mean? So, let us have a brief overview of each of these steps. When we are talking about calibration, essentially what we are referring to is that the sensor output, whatever sensor is there, to track the output of those sensors is transformed to behave as closely to the real value as possible. So the inherent assumption is that there will not be a perfect sensor.
When we are making a movement, we generate some values; sensors will capture those values, but they will not be perfectly capturing those values. So, their output will be somewhat imperfect. However, the calibration state or calibration step is meant to make or reduce that error to the extent possible. So, through this stage what we try to do is basically make the output
as close to natural or real output as possible. Then comes the registration phase or registration step. In this step, what we do is estimate or determine the initial sensor position and orientation. The idea is simple. We need to first know where exactly it starts, and then we can track the movement.
Third is the integration phase or integration step. So, in this step the aggregation or integration of sensory measurements are done at discrete points of time. Remember that we started by assuming that there will always be some error when we are measuring something with sensors or trackers. So, it can never give you exactly the natural values.
